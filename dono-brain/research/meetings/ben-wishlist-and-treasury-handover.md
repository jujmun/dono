# Meeting with Ben — Wish-List Concept & Treasury Handover

### Platform Overview and Fit

- Goal confirmed: gathering feedback, not selling
- Core concept: student "wish list" for small, tangible needs (lab coat, textbook, laptop deposit)
  - Alumni browse and fund specific items, e.g. "£15 for Sashank's lab coat"
  - Addresses the problem that large nebulous campaigns (£30k building) feel unachievable for new donors
- Key differentiator from existing crowdfunding pages: student-facing, item-level specificity
- Aesthetic praised: receipt-style format, slick design, clear cost breakdown per item

### Fundraising Logistics and Treasury Process

- Donors cannot pay directly into student bank accounts: no audit trail, no gift aid eligibility
- All donations must route through a single college bank account or Stripe, then reconciled manually
  - Stripe captures donor email and amount; treasury holds funds in a holding pot until reconciled
  - Development office cross-references Stripe data with donor details to thank and track donors
  - Treasury then allocates funds as individual grants, requiring each student's bank details
- Gift aid is a legal declaration: platform must capture name, address, and email to cross-check HMRC eligibility
- Each student disbursement is a separate manual allocation by treasury, regardless of total pot size
  - 100 donations to 100 students = 100 manual distributions: a significant treasury burden
- Donor fatigue risk: need a vetting mechanism and upper limit per request (e.g. cap single asks)
- Timing conflict: major giving day campaign planned for October/November (Rats and Tartar Building)
  - Launching simultaneously would create competing messaging
  - Recommendation: use the summer to finalise payment logistics and onboard students

## 1. Read this first

1.1 **Dono does not hold your money.** Your Donation goes directly to the Recipient's own Stripe connected account. Dono takes a platform fee and never receives, holds, safeguards or controls the donation itself.

1.2 **Dono determines whether, under the Platform's rules, a Campaign Owner is required to issue a refund.** Dono is deciding whether the Campaign Owner has complied with their obligations — it is not awarding damages. **A refund the Donor is entitled to is executed by the Recipient from their own Stripe account. Dono does not initiate refunds of Donations and holds no reserve power to do so.** Dono has sanctions and escalation routes, but **cannot compel a refund to happen**, cannot recall funds already paid out, and never pays refunds from its own funds.

1.3 Each Donation creates a direct contract between the Donor and the Campaign Owner about the use of donated funds and any refund obligations. That contract is formed on the standing-offer and agency basis in clause 12 of the Terms of Service. Dono is not a party to it; Dono administers the Platform and this process under its own Terms.

1.4 Nothing here removes your statutory rights, your rights against your card issuer, or your rights under the Payment Provider's terms.

## 2. Two different routes

2.1 A **refund request** goes to Dono, which investigates and determines whether the Campaign Owner must refund; the Recipient executes it. A **chargeback** goes to your bank or card issuer, which decides under card-scheme rules; Dono only supplies evidence and cannot stop it.

2.2 **Please contact Dono first.** In most cases we can investigate and, where appropriate, arrange a refund more quickly than a card dispute. Using Dono's process does not prevent a later chargeback, and **using a lawful chargeback does not breach these Terms.** A chargeback should generally be a last resort if the issue cannot be resolved directly with us — this is guidance, not a restriction. Note that our 21-day target may consume part of your issuer's chargeback window; if timing matters, check your issuer's deadlines.

2.3 Give accurate information in any refund request or chargeback. Knowingly making a false or fraudulent claim may result in suspension and other appropriate action.

## 3. Grounds for a refund

Refund grounds fall into two classes, and the test that applies depends on the class.

3.1 **Objective grounds.** These are established by the fact itself. **They do not require the Donor to show that the issue materially affected their decision to donate, or any causal connection to their reliance.** They are:

(a) duplicate payment;
(b) payment error, including a charge for the wrong amount;
(c) unauthorised payment;
(d) **a Donation made by a child without the permission of a parent or guardian** (Donor Terms clause 2.2);
(e) cancellation of the Campaign before the money is spent;
(f) failure or withdrawal of the Campaign Owner's student-status check, where the Campaign should not have been published; and
(g) Surplus Funds falling to be refunded under clause 10.

3.2 **Conduct grounds.** These depend on something the Campaign Owner did or failed to do, and the test in clause 4.2 applies. They are:

(a) material misrepresentation, including an untrue statement about matched funding;
(b) fraud;
(c) misuse of funds;
(d) failure to provide required evidence;
(e) a Material Change made without authorisation, or a Material Change made after funds were spent in order to regularise that spending;
(f) inability to use the money meaningfully for the stated purpose;
(g) failure to proceed where funds should be returned; and
(h) failure to publish a Closure Statement after reminders.

3.3 **Not** grounds on their own: disappointment with the outcome; a Project attempted honestly that did not succeed; minor deviations; a change of mind; or the target not being met where the money can still be used meaningfully. Campaigns describe the **intended** use of funds; they are not promises of a result.

3.4 **More than one ground may apply.** Where a request engages several grounds, all are considered — the reviewer does not have to choose one.

## 4. Decision framework

4.1 Decisions are made on the **balance of probabilities** (more likely than not), considering all relevant evidence and weighing conflicting evidence. The reviewer relies on the evidence actually available — campaign content, updates, evidence submitted, Payment Provider records and any response from the Campaign Owner — not on assumption.

4.2 **Materiality and causation apply only to conduct grounds (clause 3.2).** For those grounds, a refund is justified where the issue **materially affected** the donation or campaign outcome and there is a **causal connection** between the alleged breach and the refund requested. **Neither test is applied to an objective ground under clause 3.1** — a duplicate payment is refunded because it is a duplicate, not because the Donor can show it changed their mind.

4.3 Dono may decide on no refund, a partial refund or a full refund. A partial refund reflects the proportion of the Donation affected: where part of a Campaign proceeded as described and part did not, the refund is the proportion attributable to the part that did not, and the reasoning is recorded. Cases of shared responsibility are apportioned. Similar cases are decided consistently.

4.4 **Conflicts of interest.** Any member of the Dono team involved in a dispute, or who knows a party personally, or who donated to, shared or promoted the campaign, must declare it and recuse themselves from deciding it. The recusal is recorded with the case.

## 5. Time, completeness and the process

5.1 A request is submitted through the Platform, or by email to **joindono.team@gmail.com**, with a reason and any supporting material. A request is **complete** once Dono has the information reasonably required to assess it; if more is needed, Dono will normally identify everything it reasonably requires in a **single** request, as soon as reasonably practicable, and seeks more only if genuinely necessary.

5.2 **Time limits** (for Dono's voluntary internal process only; they do not shorten your legal rights): an ordinary request within **60 days** after the Campaign End Date, or after you became aware of the matter, whichever is later; a fraud or deliberate-misrepresentation request up to **12 months** after the Donation. Dono may accept late cases where fair; latent-fraud and legal rights are preserved.

5.3 **Timescales.** Dono aims to acknowledge a request within **two Working Days**. Dono decides within **21 calendar days** of receiving a complete request. Where a decision cannot reasonably be made in that time because of complexity or delay in obtaining information, Dono may extend the period **once by up to a further 21 calendar days**, notifying the parties of the reason and an updated expected date. **External deadlines — card-scheme chargeback windows, statutory limitation periods and any other external deadlines — continue independently of Dono's process, and Dono will tell both parties so.**

5.4 **The Campaign Owner's response.** The Campaign Owner is told the substance of the allegation and shown the evidence relied on, and has **ten Working Days** to respond. Dono will extend that period on reasonable request where the Campaign Owner is unwell, on holiday, sitting examinations or otherwise genuinely unable to respond. Urgent interim measures may be taken where there is a genuine and immediate risk. No adverse inference is drawn unless the Campaign Owner received the notice and had a fair opportunity to respond.

5.5 **Identity.** Where the Donor hid their name, Dono withholds their identity from the Campaign Owner and does not disclose it as a dispute-resolution step. Disclosure occurs only where required or permitted by law, following clause 25 of the Terms of Service (minimum necessary, documented necessity and proportionality, legal review, notice to the affected user where lawful). This does not affect what the Payment Provider separately shows the Connected Account holder; the Campaign Terms prohibit using that information to identify, contact or pressure a Donor.

5.6 Both parties receive the outcome and reasons. **An appeal may be brought within ten Working Days** under the single appeal framework in clause 8 of the Community Guidelines. The appeal is Dono's final internal decision and does not affect your statutory, card-issuer or legal rights.

## 6. How an approved refund is paid

6.1 **The Recipient executes the refund from their own Stripe dashboard.** Dono does not initiate or process the refund of a Donation through the Payment Provider's API, and holds no reserve power to do so.

6.2 A Campaign Owner notified that a refund is required is contractually obliged to execute it promptly. Failure is a breach of the Campaign Terms and a ground for enforcement under Part 10 of the Terms of Service. A refund may be delayed or fail where the Connected Account holds insufficient funds; **Dono does not make up the difference.**

6.3 **Refunds and exchange rates.** A refund returns the sterling amount donated; a non-UK card issuer converts it back at the refund-day rate, so the sum received in the Donor's currency may differ slightly. Neither Dono nor the Campaign Owner controls that rate, and neither receives any part of an issuer's charge.

## 7. If the Campaign Owner does not cooperate

7.1 Where a refund the Donor is entitled to is not executed, Dono may, in escalating order: suspend or remove the Campaign; suspend or permanently ban the account; prevent future fundraising on Dono; escalate to the Recognised Institution through Dono's institutional contact channel under clause 23.7 of the Terms of Service and record that correspondence; support the Donor's chargeback by assembling evidence; and report suspected fraud to the police or another authority.

7.2 **Dono cannot withhold, delay or divert a payout.** Under the live Payment Provider configuration Dono has no ability to hold funds in, or release funds from, a Connected Account, and this Policy claims no such power.

7.3 Dono does not pursue debt collection and does not guarantee recovery. The Campaign Owner remains contractually liable to the Donor, who may pursue chargeback, Payment Provider or legal remedies, including through appropriate legal channels where they need the Campaign Owner's identity.

## 8. Chargebacks and disputes

8.1 A chargeback is decided by the Donor's card issuer under network rules; neither Dono nor the Payment Provider makes the substantive decision, and Dono cannot prevent or reverse one.

8.2 **Under the live configuration:** the **Connected Account bears the chargeback**, is charged the Payment Provider's dispute fee (currently **£20** per dispute in the UK), and **owns the dispute** — meaning the Connected Account holder is the party who submits evidence to the Payment Provider. Where a negative balance cannot be recovered from the Connected Account, the Payment Provider is the losses collector under Dono's configuration and absorbs the unrecoverable remainder.

8.3 **Dono's role in a dispute is to assist, not to decide or to submit on the Campaign Owner's behalf.** Dono will provide the records it holds — campaign content as it appeared, evidence submitted, correspondence, the refund determination and its reasons — promptly on request, so that the Connected Account holder can submit them. ## 9. Fees on a refund

9.1 Dono's platform fee is the Payment Provider's applicable cost plus 3.5 percentage points, of which the 3.5 percentage points is Dono's own fee (clause 16 of the Terms of Service).

9.2 **Dono refunds its own platform fee where a refund is due and the Donor was not at fault.** Applying that: where a Campaign cannot proceed, or the Campaign Owner caused the refund through mistake, breach or misrepresentation, the Donor receives Dono's fee back and the Campaign Owner bears the economic cost so far as technically possible; where the Donor caused the refund through their own error, Dono's fee is not refunded; on a partial refund, Dono's fee is refunded proportionately where the Donor was not at fault. A fee-cover amount the Donor paid is treated the same way as Dono's fee.

9.3 **How this works mechanically.** There are two separate movements. First, the Recipient refunds the charge from their Connected Account — that is the Donor's contribution coming back. Second, **Dono refunds its application fee through the Payment Provider's application-fee mechanism**, proportionally where the refund is partial; this is Dono returning its own money and is the only part of the process Dono executes. The Payment Provider's own processing cost is generally **not** returned by the Payment Provider on a refund and is borne by the Connected Account. The published clause states separately: what the Donor actually receives; who bears the economic cost; and what the Payment Provider can technically reverse.

## 10. Failed, cancelled and partially funded Campaigns; surplus

10.1 Not reaching target does not automatically cancel a Project; the Campaign Owner decides whether it can still proceed meaningfully, otherwise refunds are arranged. A Campaign cancelled before the money is spent is refunded.

10.2 **Surplus.** Any amount that cannot reasonably be used for the stated purpose is refunded in **reverse chronological order** — the most recent Donor first, up to the amount they donated, then the next, until the surplus is exhausted. Surplus is **not** shared proportionately, and no Donor is guaranteed a share; **this is disclosed at checkout before the Donor pays** and again in the Closure Statement. Dono maintains a ledger recording every surplus refund and its order.

10.3 **De minimis.** **A Campaign cannot be funded beyond its target, so over-funding does not arise and no de minimis applies to it — any over-payment is refunded in full.** Surplus arising from **under-spend** is different: a residual amount below a stated threshold need not be refunded, because the Payment Provider's fixed per-transaction charge can make a very small refund cost more than the refund itself. Where a residual amount is retained under this clause it **remains dedicated to the Campaign's stated purpose and must be applied to it by the Campaign Owner. It is never retained by Dono as revenue**, and it must be declared in the Closure Statement. **[TO BE CALCULATED BY DONO TEAM — set the threshold by reference to the objective per-transaction cost; disclose it at checkout and closure.]**

## 11. Evidence, updates and closure

11.1 Campaign Owners declare expected purchase dates, submit receipts privately within 14 days of expenditure (redacted per the Campaign Terms; Dono may itself redact or remove unnecessary personal data), provide updates, and submit a Closure Statement under clause 10.3 of the Terms of Service — 30-day submission, defined contents, 30-day review, limited rejection grounds, one cure cycle, and **administrative closure** where Dono does not decide in time. **Administrative closure is not a finding that the Campaign Owner complied** and does not prejudice a Donor's refund rights.

11.2 What Donors see is the factual evidence status only: "Documents received; line-item amounts matched to budget; documents not authenticated", *evidence outstanding*, or *evidence overdue*. Failure to provide evidence and failure to publish a Closure Statement are conduct grounds under clause 3.2 and grounds for enforcement.

## 12. Complaints and records

12.1 A refund request is only one kind of complaint. Complaints about other matters — account suspension, campaign removal, verification, technical issues, moderation, fees, or how a refund request was **handled** as opposed to how it was decided — are dealt with under Dono's separate **Complaints Policy**, referenced in clause 33 of the Terms of Service. All complaints go to the same address: **joindono.team@gmail.com**.

12.2 **Records.** Dono retains refund and dispute records (requests, evidence, decisions, reasons, appeals, escalation) and receipts and evidence of expenditure in accordance with the **retention schedule in the Privacy Notice**, which sets a separate period for each category rather than a single blanket period. Refund and dispute records and uploaded expenditure evidence are retained for six years from case closure or campaign completion, reflecting the limitation period for contract claims in England and Wales.

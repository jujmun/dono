> **This is a Notice, not a contract.** It explains Dono's checks and the statuses shown on the Platform. It does not create contractual rights or obligations. Operative obligations sit in the Student and Society Campaign Terms.

> **What changed from v2.1:** the **age position** is stated accurately — 18+ for accounts, no age restriction on donating, and age is **declared, not verified**; the section on the Payment Provider's checks records that its identity check does **not** reliably return a date of birth and is not used as Dono's age gate; **"institution verification" is expressly disclaimed** because Dono performs no such check; **society approval is described as one approver with scaled evidence**, matching the Society Campaign Terms; and the contact address is standardised.

## 1. Purpose

1.1 The word "verified" means different things to different people, so this Notice explains exactly what Dono checks, what it does not, what others check, and what each status shown on the Platform means. **Nothing on the Platform should lead a Donor to believe Dono has confirmed more than it actually has.**

## 2. One mandatory validation before publication

2.1 **Every Campaign completes the same standard validation before it can be published, and every Campaign is reviewed by a person.** There are no "Verified Student" or "Verified Campaign" badges. The validation checks whether a Campaign meets Dono's publication requirements — it is not an endorsement, guarantee, audit or certification.

2.2 The validation consists of:

(a) **identity and student-status checking** (clause 3);
(b) **required documents** for the campaign type;
(c) **campaign completeness** — that the required fields (clause 7.1 of the Terms of Service, including the Ownership Statement) are present and internally consistent;
(d) a **content review** against the Community Guidelines, carried out with the knowledge that campaign pages can be viewed by people of any age; and
(e) for Society Campaigns, **society approval** and the authority declaration (clause 2 of the Society Campaign Terms).

2.3 Publication means only that these requirements were met. It does **not** confirm the accuracy of campaign statements, the future use of funds, or the authenticity of any supporting document.

## 3. The student-status check

3.1 To create a Campaign an applicant provides a valid university email address at a Recognised Institution, an image of a current student card (for one-off checking only), their name, declared date of birth, institution and college, course, student number, card expiry date, and Course End Date.

3.2 A Dono administrator manually reviews the card image against the information provided and the university email, aiming to complete review within seven Working Days (a target, not a commitment). Dono may request further information (respond within ten Working Days, with an extension available on request) and may refuse a check where it reasonably suspects fraud, impersonation or circumvention.

3.3 **What Dono keeps.** Dono retains the **student-card number** and the extracted details it needs for eligibility, disputes and any institutional referral. **Dono deletes the student-card image immediately after a successful check** — see the Privacy Notice for the exact retention rules and safeguards. **[ENGINEERING — BUILD REQUIRED: automatic deletion is not yet implemented; this Notice must not be published until it is.]**

3.4 A completed check does **not** mean Dono has verified or guaranteed that any campaign statement is true, that any document is authentic, that any cost is accurate or reasonable, that the Project is viable or lawful in every respect, how the Campaign Owner will behave, that funds will be used as promised, that any institution or society endorses the Campaign, or that the Campaign will succeed. Donors must form their own judgement.

3.5 **Dono does not carry out "institution verification".** Dono does not contact a university or college to confirm a student's affiliation, and no institution confirms anything about a Campaign to Dono as part of the standard process. What Dono does is check a student card and a university email address, by eye, itself. Where a Dono document elsewhere refers to institutional confirmation, it means the reporting and enquiry route in clause 23 of the Terms of Service, which is not part of the publication check.

## 4. The Payment Provider's checks, and age

4.1 Every Campaign Owner completes the Payment Provider's onboarding for a Stripe Connect connected account before a Campaign can receive Donations. Those identity, KYC and eligibility checks are performed by the Payment Provider, under its own terms, for its own regulatory purposes — not for Dono. Dono does not control them and **does not receive the identity documents the Payment Provider collects**. Where the Payment Provider returns a verified name or date of birth to Dono, Dono records it; that does not happen in every flow.

4.2 The Payment Provider's onboarding status is recorded internally and **not** shown publicly: every live Campaign has completed it, so a public label would convey nothing while wrongly implying the Payment Provider has assessed the Campaign.

4.3 **Age — stated plainly.**

- **You must be 18 or over to create a Dono account**, and therefore to create or operate a Campaign, act for a Society, or post a comment.
- **There is no age restriction on browsing, and none on donating.** Anyone may view a public campaign page, and anyone may donate, with or without an account. At checkout we ask you to confirm that you are 18 or over, or that you have a parent or guardian's permission.
- **Age is declared, not verified.** Dono asks for your date of birth and relies on your answer. **A declared date of birth is not highly effective age assurance**, and Dono does not claim it is. The student card is not relied on as proof of age. The Payment Provider's identity check does not reliably return a date of birth in every flow, so Dono does not use it as an age gate.

Because children can reach the Platform, Dono has completed a children's access assessment and a children's risk assessment under the Online Safety Act 2023, and operates the safety measures described in the Community Guidelines rather than relying on an age restriction it does not enforce. **[SOLICITOR SIGN-OFF — children's access assessment and children's risk assessment.]**

## 5. Society approval

5.1 A Society Campaign is approved through the relevant society page before it is presented as a society campaign. Society approval means only that the Society approved the Campaign for that presentation. It does **not** mean the institution endorses it, that the Society verified the campaign's contents, that the Society bears legal responsibility, or that the Society is a separate legal person.

5.2 **One approver is sufficient for Dono to publish.** The Society registers one named **Society Representative**, who confirms authority to act, and one named **Secondary Contact**, who may take over if the primary leaves office. **The Secondary Contact is not a second approver.** Separately, and as evidence that the Society itself authorised the Campaign under its own rules, more evidence is required as the funding target rises — a confirmation under £2,500, two officers' written confirmation between £2,500 and £10,000, and formal committee approval above £10,000 (clause 2.3 of the Society Campaign Terms). That is evidence of the Society's internal authorisation, not a second Dono approval.

## 6. Institutional endorsement

6.1 "Institutionally endorsed" is used **only** where the Recognised Institution has expressly endorsed that specific Campaign — not because a student used a university email, the institution is listed, a society is associated with it, or a campaign mentions it.

## 7. Statuses shown on the Platform

7.1 Dono shows **plainly worded, factual statements**, not a single combined or unexplained "Verified" badge. Where displayed, and only where accurate, these may include: "Student status manually checked on [date] — campaign claims not checked"; "Society approved"; and "Institutionally endorsed". Each links to this Notice. No status may be presented, and no marketing may suggest, that Dono guarantees a Campaign, its authenticity or its outcome.

7.2 **Evidence status.** Separately, each campaign page shows one of: **"Documents received; line-item amounts matched to budget; documents not authenticated"**; *evidence outstanding*; or *evidence overdue*. The first is a record of an administrative review only — it does not confirm that goods were delivered, that a price was reasonable, that funds were used properly, or that any document is authentic. Receipts themselves are held privately and not shown to Donors.

7.3 Recorded internally and not shown publicly: Payment Provider onboarding status; investigation outcomes; and internal risk classifications.

## 8. Reverification and changes in student status

8.1 Student status is reverified each October, or on the card expiry date if earlier; a check within the preceding three months defers reverification to the next cycle. Dono also reverifies status at Campaign creation. While reverification is pending and in time, a live Campaign runs normally. Where a university email address stops working or Dono has reason to doubt a Campaign Owner's status, Dono may pause the Campaign pending a check.

8.2 The **obligations** on a Campaign Owner to remain eligible, to notify interruption, leave or suspension, and to deal with loss of status mid-campaign, are set out in the Student and Society Campaign Terms, not in this Notice.

## 9. Withdrawal of a status; reporting; contact

9.1 Dono may withdraw any status at any time (for example where status lapses, information proves inaccurate, a society withdraws approval, an institution withdraws endorsement, or the Payment Provider closes a Connected Account). Withdrawal is not by itself a finding of wrongdoing. **A refusal or withdrawal of a student-status check may be appealed within ten Working Days** under clause 8 of the Community Guidelines.

9.2 Anyone may report a Campaign they believe inaccurate or in breach, using the report control on the Campaign or the public reporting form — no account is needed (Community Guidelines clause 7).

# Dono legal suite v2.2 — change log & coverage

**Date:** 31 July 2026
**Basis:** the pre-launch counsel review (30 July 2026), the founder's amendments, the engineering configuration answers of 31 July 2026 (`developer-configuration-form.md`, `cookie-configuration-review.md`, `terms-engineering-questionnaire-answers.md`), and sixteen founder decisions recorded on 31 July 2026.
**Method:** `terms_v2.1/` was left completely untouched. Revised documents were written into `terms_v2.2/`. The full list of inconsistencies this version resolves is in `legal/v2.1-reconciliation-report.md` — 92 items, 72 blocking and 20 cosmetic.

> **Still not for publication.** Every document requires final UK solicitor sign-off, and a substantial number of clauses describe controls that **have not been built**. Those are marked **[ENGINEERING — BUILD REQUIRED]** in the text and collected in `TASKS_engineering_features.md`. Publishing a clause describing a control that does not exist would turn an aspiration into a representation.

---

## What made v2.2 necessary

v2.1 was drafted before engineering answered how the product actually works. When those answers arrived, they contradicted the Terms on several points of fact — not of drafting. **Nine documents stated that Dono could not initiate a refund while the code already did; six claimed a power to withhold payouts that does not exist and is architecturally forbidden; the fee model in the Terms was not the fee model in the code; and two categories on the prohibited list were shipped features.** v2.2 is largely the work of making the documents true.

The second driver was the age position, which v2.1 stated three different ways across public and internal documents. It is now settled and stated identically everywhere.

## Where v2.2 knowingly departs from the review

Recorded so the departures are visible rather than buried:

1. **Commercial campaigns** are permitted with a firm no-benefit rule, where the review preferred prohibition at beta.
2. **Geography** relies on a not-targeted statement and a reserved right to restrict, where the review preferred an approved-country list with geoblocking.
3. **Surplus** remains reverse-chronological, where the review preferred pro-rata.
4. **Refunds** remain owner-executed, where the review preferred platform-initiated refunds with reserves. The review's fallback applies: the practical limitation is now made prominent before donation.
5. **CSEA retention** follows the 2026 Regulations' two clocks rather than the review's single "five-year record schedule", which was wrong.

## What still blocks publication

1. **Engineering.** Roughly two dozen clauses describe controls that do not exist — see `TASKS_engineering_features.md`. The retention schedule, the student-card deletion job, the report controls, the checkout age confirmation, the durable copy and the fee-model change are the load-bearing ones.
2. **Solicitor sign-off** on the whole suite, especially: the payment-services perimeter; contract formation and agency; society capacity and the personal-fallback clause; consumer status of student campaign owners; the business-user cap; the Consumer Contracts Regulations position; and the DMCC Act section 308 information.
3. **Three DPAs** (Resend, PostHog, Stripe) and the **Vercel transfer risk assessment**.
4. **NCA CSEA portal registration** and tested access for Amrit and Sashank.
5. **The de minimis figure** for under-spend surplus.
6. **Team agreements** — confidentiality, IP assignment and data-handling — for everyone with access to identity data, evidence, moderation records or appeals.
7. **The incident-response tabletop exercise.**
8. **The pre-launch acceptance test** in the Online Safety Act Procedures.

Only once these are closed should the DRAFT banners be removed, document by document, against a sign-off register recording for each: owner, evidence, decision, approver and the document version it was applied to.

### 1. Purpose and scope

1.1 These Community Guidelines apply **contractually to account holders and to anyone who submits content or uses the community features** of Dono (comments, reports, campaign pages, images, documents and usernames). They form part of your agreement with Dono when you take one of those actions.

1.2 The reporting, complaints and appeals routes in clauses 7 and 8 are open to **anyone**, including people who do not hold a Dono account and people who have never used Dono. Those clauses describe how Dono operates; they do not require you to be a party to any contract.

1.3 Dono is a platform for genuine student fundraising within a university community. These Guidelines keep it honest, respectful, lawful and safe. **Dono's public pages can be viewed by anyone of any age**, so these Guidelines and the moderation process apply with that audience in mind.

### 2. Core principles

2.1 Be honest: campaigns and comments must not mislead. Be respectful: disagreement is fine; abuse is not. Be lawful: nothing may facilitate or promote illegal activity. Be safe: do not endanger, exploit or target others.

### 3. Prohibited content and conduct

3.1 The following are prohibited anywhere on the Platform. Dono removes them and may suspend or ban the account responsible.

**Illegal content.** Content that is, or that encourages or assists, any of the following is prohibited without exception:

- terrorism content, and fundraising for or promotion of a proscribed organisation;
- child sexual abuse material, and any content that sexualises, grooms or exploits a child;
- content encouraging or assisting suicide or serious self-harm;
- intimate images shared without consent, and cyberflashing;
- threats to kill, harassment, stalking and controlling or coercive behaviour;
- hate offences — content that is threatening or abusive and intended to stir up hatred on the grounds of race, religion, sexual orientation, disability or transgender identity;
- fraud and financial-services offences, including invented beneficiaries, impersonated societies, falsified evidence and coordinated fake comments;
- proceeds of crime, money laundering and sanctions evasion;
- the sale or supply of drugs, psychoactive substances, firearms, knives or other unlawful goods;
- human trafficking, sexual exploitation and facilitating unlawful immigration;
- extreme pornography;
- animal cruelty; and
- foreign interference offences.

**Also prohibited:** misleading fundraising; fundraising on behalf of another individual or for a third-party beneficiary; pass-through campaigns; duplicate or deceptive campaigns; plagiarism; harassment and abuse falling short of an offence; discrimination; impersonation; spam; sexual or pornographic content; content that is grossly offensive; dangerous or regulated activity; copyright and trade-mark infringement; unauthorised use of institutional branding; false claims of affiliation or endorsement; ban evasion; and abusive reporting under clause 7.5.

3.2 **What "grossly offensive" and "dangerous" mean.** These are judged by what a reasonable person would think, not by whether someone was upset. *Grossly offensive* means content whose main effect is to degrade, dehumanise or humiliate — for example content mocking a person's disability, or celebrating a death. *Dangerous* means content likely to lead a reader to physical harm — for example instructions for a hazardous stunt, or a campaign promoting an activity without the safety measures the activity obviously requires. Strong opinions, criticism of a campaign, and blunt disagreement are not prohibited.

3.3 **How we enforce.** We take account of how serious the content is, whether it was deliberate, whether it caused or risked harm, whether it targeted a particular person, and whether it is part of a pattern. The proportionate outcome may be no action, a warning, removing or hiding the content, restricting a feature, restricting the Campaign during an investigation, suspending the account, removing the Campaign, or a permanent ban.

### 4. Sensitive personal information

4.1 Do not publish another person's medical records, diagnosis letters, passport, driving licence, criminal allegations, disciplinary findings, religious information, political opinions or sexual orientation, unless you have the legal right to do so and it is genuinely necessary.

4.2 Do not upload unnecessary sensitive material about yourself either — for example MRI scans, diagnosis letters or identity documents — where the campaign can be made without it.

4.3 **Dono may remove or redact sensitive information even where the Campaign itself remains online**, and may request evidence privately rather than allowing it to be published.

### 5. Campaign integrity and factual statements

5.1 Campaigns must comply with clause 8 of the Terms of Service and the applicable Campaign Terms. Do not create duplicate or deceptive campaigns, fundraise for another individual, run pass-through campaigns, plagiarise, or claim a check, approval or endorsement you do not have.

5.2 Any status shown on a campaign page (for example "Student status manually checked on [date]", "Society approved", or an evidence status) describes a specific, limited, factual check. **Dono does not use "Verified" badges and none of these is a guarantee of a campaign.** Do not misdescribe them.

5.3 **Matched funding.** If a Campaign says a third party will match donations, the statement must be true, the matching party must be named, and the amount, ratio, period and any cap must be shown. Dono requires evidence of the commitment before a Match Window opens. A false or exaggerated matching claim is misleading fundraising.

### 6. Comments

6.1 There is no private messaging; all comments are public and display a name. **You must have an account, and therefore be 18 or over, to post a comment.** Hidden-name commenting is not available. You may edit or delete your own comments; the interface shows when a comment was edited. Campaign owners may remove a comment from their own campaign but not edit another user's wording; Dono keeps a private moderation copy and may restore a comment where removal is misused. There is no blocking feature; use reporting instead.

6.2 **Links and attachments in comments are not permitted.** Comments are plain text. This is a deliberate safety choice: links and attachments are a common route for scams, phishing and harmful material, and comments are visible to people of any age. **[ENGINEERING — confirm links and attachments are technically disabled in comments, not merely discouraged.]**

### 7. Reporting

7.1 **A report control appears on every item of user content** — campaign pages, campaign images and uploaded documents, comments, and usernames. For a document, the report control appears beside the document on the campaign page. **[ENGINEERING — BUILD REQUIRED: report controls do not yet appear on every surface.]**

7.2 **You do not need an account to report.** A publicly accessible reporting form at **/report** is available without logging in, and asks for: the campaign or content link; the type of content; why you are concerned; whether you are personally affected; an email address if you want updates (optional); and anything else you think relevant. You may also email **joindono.team@gmail.com**. **[ENGINEERING — BUILD REQUIRED: the public reporting form does not yet exist.]**

7.3 **Report categories.** You do not have to decide whether a criminal offence has occurred. Choose whichever of these fits best:

- scam, fraud or misleading fundraising;
- threats, harassment or hate;
- terrorism or violent extremism;
- child sexual abuse or exploitation;
- sexual or intimate images shared without consent;
- encouraging suicide or serious self-harm;
- drugs, weapons or other unlawful goods;
- privacy or personal-information concern;
- content harmful to children; or
- something else.

**If you are reporting suspected child sexual abuse material, please do not download, copy, screenshot or attach the material.** Give us the Dono link or the campaign identifier and we will take it from there.

7.4 **What we do with a report.** We acknowledge it, triage it by urgency, and review the content against these Guidelines, the Terms and the law. We may conclude either that we have reasonable grounds to consider the content illegal, **or** that we cannot confidently determine legality but the content breaches these Guidelines or the Terms — either conclusion is enough for us to act. We act swiftly to remove or restrict content we determine to be illegal. We notify the affected user where lawful and safe to do so, allow a counter-notice where appropriate, apply repeat-infringer controls, preserve records of the decision and its reasons, and report child sexual exploitation and abuse content to the National Crime Agency. Reports that suggest an immediate danger to someone are escalated straight away and outside the ordinary queue.

7.5 **Abusive reporting.** A report that we do not uphold is **not** abusive, and you will never be penalised for a report made in good faith that turns out to be mistaken. What is prohibited is reporting content you **know** to be permissible, reporting recklessly, or reporting repeatedly in order to harass someone or to disrupt a campaign.

### 8. Complaints and appeals — the single framework

8.1 This clause is Dono's complaints and appeals framework for content and moderation. **Every other Dono document points here**, so the process is the same wherever you read about it. Complaints about other things — fees, technical faults, delays, verification, or how a process was handled — go to the Complaints Policy. The substantive decision on whether a refund is due is made under the Refund and Dispute Policy.

8.2 **Who may complain, and about what.**

| You are… | You may challenge… | Deadline | Called |
|---|---|---|---|
| A person whose content, campaign or account was moderated | removal, hiding, restriction, suspension, a warning, or a ban | 10 Working Days from being told | Appeal |
| A person who reported content | Dono's failure to remove or restrict content you reported, or how your report was handled | 10 Working Days from the outcome | Complaint |
| Anyone, including a non-user | that Dono is not complying with its illegal-content, child-safety, reporting, complaints or freedom-of-expression duties | no deadline | Complaint |
| A child, or an adult on a child's behalf | anything above, including content harmful to children | as above | Complaint or Appeal |
| A person refused or withdrawn a student-status check | the refusal or withdrawal | 10 Working Days | Appeal |
| A Donor or Campaign Owner in a refund case | the refund determination | 10 Working Days | Appeal (Refund and Dispute Policy clause 5.6) |

8.3 **How to submit.** Use the appeal link in the message telling you about the decision, or email **joindono.team@gmail.com**. Say which decision or report you are challenging, why, and give any new information. This is the only address; it is monitored and every complaint is logged.

8.4 **Timescales.** We acknowledge within **five Working Days**. We aim to give an outcome within **30 days** of receipt. If it will take longer, we tell you why and when to expect it. Urgent matters — anything involving an immediate risk to a person, child safety, or suspected illegal content — are handled straight away and outside these timescales.

8.5 **Who decides.** An appeal or complaint is reviewed by **a member of the Dono team who was not substantially involved in the original decision**, applying conflict-of-interest and recusal rules. Amrit Kaur Rooprai is the accountable person for content and safety decisions and is the final escalation point; where she made the original decision, another member of the team conducts the review first. **Where the size of the team makes separation impossible, the original decision-maker may reconsider — but only as a documented, genuine second look at the original evidence and any new information you provide, recorded as such.** We do not promise independence we cannot deliver. For particularly complex or high-risk cases we may take external legal or specialist advice.

8.6 **Possible outcomes.** The original decision may be **confirmed, varied or reversed**. Where it is reversed we restore the content, the campaign's functionality or account access so far as reasonably possible, and we record the reason.

8.7 **Records.** We record every report, complaint and appeal — the date received, the category, the acknowledgement date, the decision and reasons, the outcome, and any reversal — and review them monthly to spot recurring problems.

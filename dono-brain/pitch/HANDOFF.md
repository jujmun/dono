## 2. The task

Ten-minute investor pitch for a university competition, plus Q&A. Judges are described as "friendly but informed" — expect a CEO, a senior businessperson, and lawyers from Mishcon de Reya. Assume at least one person in the room has run a fundraising operation.

**Scoring — 10 points each, plus a 5-point tie-break:**

| Criterion | Notes |
|---|---|
| Quality of pitch — clarity, visual aids, professionalism | |
| **Understanding of the market** | **Our strongest, winnable outright** |
| Business proposition coherence | |
| Team dynamic and chemistry | Q&A discipline matters as much as the team slide |
| Innovation — originality and passion | Carried by the £400 story |
| *Tie-break (5 pts): is the proposed use of funds clear and practically impactful?* | **Currently unearned — the ask slide has no figures** |

The panel also works through: is the problem real · is the market large enough · is the solution clear · is the customer easy to understand · can this make money · is there evidence people want this · does the team know what it's doing · what makes this different · how will the investment be used · what milestones will it unlock · where could this go.

## 4. The fact base (everything already extracted)

### 4.1 Student survey
**26 responses; 23 current Oxford students; 14 colleges.** Denominators below are people who answered that question. `market-research-notes.md` says n=23 throughout — numerators are right, the denominator is "answered," not "respondents."

- **14 of 23** have needed money for a university activity
- Frequency: **2–3×/year** modal (9 people); 3 more at 4–5×
- Amount: **£150–£500** modal (7); 3 at £500–£1,000; 3 at £1,000–£2,000
- **11 of 23** applied for a grant/hardship/travel fund → **8 were rejected or under-funded (73%)**
- **3 of those 8 forfeited the opportunity entirely (38% of rejected applicants)**
- Fallback: 5 own savings · 4 parents/family · 1 student loan · 1 extra paid work · **3 went without**
- **8 of 23** say their societies struggle to fund things *often or always*
- Likelihood to create a campaign: **mean 6.4/10**, 12 of 23 scored ≥7
- Usefulness of Dono: **mean 6.4/10**, 11 of 23 scored ≥7
- **Rejected applicants score 7.1 vs the 6.4 average** ← the closest thing to PMF in the dataset
- Trust: **12 of 23** say official college/university endorsement is the one thing that would make them trust Dono — more than friends using it (5), seeing outcomes (3) and fund transparency (2) *combined*
- Barriers: embarrassment **14** · "nobody would actually donate" **11** · "not for me, others need it more" 8 · effort 7 · privacy 7 · **fees only 4** (1 dealbreaker)
- Visibility: 10 college-only · 8 fully public · 2 anonymous · 1 donor-only · **only 1 wouldn't post at all**
- **7 opted into further research** (4 offered a 1-to-1)
- Named use cases with their own prices: rowing blazer · £400 research/work-experience flights · Oxformals £500 · MBAT €700 · travel ~£700 · research trip/internship abroad · learning resources (few hundred) · performing-arts society campaign

### 4.3 External market data
- **CAF UK Giving 2026:** total UK donations **£15.4bn (2024) → £14bn (2025)**; average gift **£72 → £65**; 49% of non-donors cite affordability (up from 44%)
- **~6 in 10** believe donations reach their intended cause (UK government reporting)
- **HEPI:** first-year living costs **£21,126** (England) / £24,900 (London); max maintenance loan covers ~50%
- **HEPI:** **68%** of students work during term time, up from 35% in 2015
- **NUS:** max-support parental income threshold frozen at £25,000 since 2008 (~£41,000 inflation-adjusted)
- **OxReach** (Oxford + Hubbub): **6 projects ever — 4 from professors**, 1 master's, 1 undergraduate. Not renewed

### 4.4 Business model
- Fee = **payment provider's cost + 3.5 percentage points**. The 3.5pp is Dono's revenue
- Donor may cover the fee or have it deducted. Students never pay to create a campaign. Colleges pay nothing
- **Dono never holds funds** — every donation is a Stripe Connect **direct charge** to the recipient's connected account. No pooled funds, no escrow, no platform balance. Dono cannot hold, delay or divert a payout
- Campaigns cannot be funded beyond target. Under-spend refunded in reverse chronological order

## 5. Accuracy flags — check before anything is said on stage

1. **"We have raised nothing"** is unsafe. `company/company-context.md` records grant money transferred into the Starling account (31 Jul 2026). Use *"no equity raised, no external investors."*
2. **Entity is contradictory.** `TRUTH.md` (31 Jul, authoritative) — trading name of a sole trader, no incorporation planned. `corporate/founder-context-handoff.md` (16 Jul) — five founders, UK Ltd planned. **Make no entity claim on stage until settled.** "Who owns it?" is routine Q&A.
3. **Insurance is contradictory.** `company-context.md` says added 31 Jul; `TRUTH.md` same date says uninsured sole trader. Don't claim it.
4. **Team size:** pitch plan says four founders; corporate handoff says five. Decks show four.
5. **"15+ development offices"** appears in a v1 speaker note. Only **11** are documented. Either write up the other four or say 11.
6. **73% is 8 of 11.** If pressed, say *"eight of the eleven students in our survey who applied."*
7. **Community Funds are in product docs but prohibited at launch** by `TRUTH.md` (no pooled funds). Don't mention them — a judge asking about FCA permissions would find the inconsistency.
8. **Confirm the India Society campaign is genuinely live** before claiming it. A judge who checks and finds it isn't costs more than the slide gains.

## 7. Anti-patterns

- **Don't re-read the meeting notes or CSVs** for figures that are in §4. That's the main cost this file exists to avoid
- **Don't name any college** in pitch material
- **Don't present quotes as verbatim** — they're compressed paraphrases
- **Don't rebuild the team's deck** — the agreement is notes against `Dono Pitch Deck v1.pptx`
- **Don't lead with the legal suite as traction** — "nine legal documents, zero users" reads as misallocated effort. It's a Q&A answer, not a slide
- **Don't put a top-down TAM on a slide.** Do the arithmetic verbally and let the audience build it; the defensible money argument is **cost of collection**, not pot size
- **Don't claim traction that is validation.** Lead with market understanding — it's the criterion we can win outright

# Dono Team and Contributor Agreement

**Version:** 2.3
**Version date:** 6 August 2026
**Document type:** Template agreement — to be executed by every founder, contributor, volunteer and contractor
**Owner:** Amrit Kaur Rooprai
**Resolves review finding:** F53

> **Why this exists.** Dono is a sole trader. Everything — the code, the brand, the data, the regulatory responsibility — currently sits with one person, while several people build and operate the Platform. Without written agreements, ownership of the code and brand is uncertain, there is no contractual control over who touches personal data, and a departure or disagreement could impair the product. **This is the minimum governance needed for launch, kept proportionate for an early-stage sole trader. Incorporation is not required for it to work, and this agreement is designed to survive one.**

## 1. What this agreement is

1.1 This agreement governs your contribution to Dono and your access to Dono's systems and data. It applies whether you are paid or unpaid, and whether or not you are described as a founder.

1.2 **It is not an employment contract**, does not create an employment relationship, and does not by itself give you any equity, share, option or profit entitlement. Any economic interest is a separate matter, dealt with in writing separately, and is expressly outside this agreement.

1.3 **Acceptance.** This agreement may be signed by hand, signed electronically, or accepted by email confirmation identifying this version by number and date. Dono records the version, the method and the date. **A handwritten signature is not required for this agreement to be binding.**

## 2. Intellectual property assignment

2.1 **You assign to Amrit Kaur Rooprai, absolutely and with full title guarantee, all present and future intellectual property rights in everything you create for or in connection with Dono**, throughout the world, for the full term of those rights. This includes: source code, scripts, configuration and infrastructure-as-code; database schemas and data models; designs, wireframes, prototypes and design systems; the Dono name, logo, brand assets and copy; documentation, policies and legal drafting; research, analysis and datasets you compile for Dono; and any invention or improvement.

2.2 The assignment takes effect **as the work is created**, and applies to everything you have created for Dono **before** the date of this agreement as well as afterwards.

2.3 You waive, so far as the law allows, all **moral rights** in that work.

2.4 You will, at Dono's reasonable request and cost, sign anything and do anything reasonably needed to give effect to this clause or to register, perfect or enforce any right — including after you stop contributing.

2.5 **What is not assigned.** Anything you created wholly independently of Dono, before your start date, and not using Dono's resources, remains yours ("**Background IP**"). **You must identify any Background IP you incorporate into Dono's work before you incorporate it.** Where you do, you grant Dono a perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free, sub-licensable licence to use, modify and exploit it for any purpose connected with Dono.

2.6 **Open-source components.** You must not incorporate third-party code into Dono without checking the licence is compatible with Dono's use, and recording the component, its version and its licence. You must never incorporate code that would require Dono to release its own source code.

2.7 **On incorporation.** If Dono incorporates, Amrit Kaur Rooprai may assign the benefit of this agreement, and all rights assigned under it, to the new company without your further consent. You will sign any confirmatory assignment reasonably required.

## 3. Confidentiality

3.1 **Confidential Information** means anything non-public you learn through Dono: personal data of users; source code and architecture; security arrangements, credentials and vulnerabilities; commercial terms, pricing models and financial information; investor and institutional discussions; unreleased product plans; moderation cases and their contents; legal advice; and anything a reasonable person would treat as confidential.

3.2 You must: keep it confidential; use it only for Dono's purposes; not disclose it to anyone without authority; and protect it with at least reasonable care.

3.3 **Exceptions:** information that is public other than through your breach; that you already lawfully held without obligation; that you develop independently; or that you must disclose by law or by a regulator or court — in which case you tell Dono first, unless you are prohibited from doing so.

3.4 **Confidentiality survives indefinitely** for personal data and security information, and for **three years** after you stop contributing for everything else.

3.5 **Public statements.** You must not speak publicly for Dono — press, social media, conference talks, investor conversations — without the founder's authority, and must not disclose an incident, a user matter or a legal matter publicly at any time.

## 4. Data protection and authorised access

4.1 **Dono (Amrit Kaur Rooprai) is the data controller.** You process personal data only **on Dono's documented instructions** and only for the purposes of your role.

4.2 **You must:**

(a) access personal data **only where you need it for a task you have been asked to do** — not out of curiosity, not to look up someone you know, not to check on a campaign you are interested in;
(b) access only the **minimum** necessary;
(c) never copy personal data to a personal device, personal email, personal cloud storage, or any unapproved tool;
(d) never export a dataset without authority;
(e) never share personal data with anyone outside Dono without authority;
(f) never use personal data for your own purposes, including research, marketing or a project of your own;
(g) never attempt to re-identify anonymised or aggregated data;
(h) **tell the incident lead immediately** if you become aware of any actual or suspected breach, loss, unauthorised access or accidental disclosure — **immediately means the same hour, not the next working day**;
(i) cooperate with any data-subject request, complaint or investigation; and
(j) complete the data-protection and security induction before your first access, and any refresher Dono requires.

4.3 **Restricted categories.** You must not access special category data, criminal-offence data, safeguarding records, moderation case contents or suspected CSEA material unless your role expressly requires it and you have been specifically authorised. **Access to those categories is logged.**

4.4 **CSEA material.** If you encounter suspected child sexual abuse material you must follow the CSEA Detection, Preservation and Reporting Procedure exactly: do not download, copy, screenshot or forward it; note the identifiers; restrict it in place; escalate immediately. **Mishandling this material can be a criminal offence.**

4.5 **Monitoring.** Dono logs access to production systems and personal data, and may review those logs to check compliance with this clause. You consent to that monitoring for that purpose.

## 5. Security responsibilities

5.1 You must:

(a) use **multi-factor authentication** on every account that can reach Dono's systems or data where the service offers it, including your Dono email, GitHub, Vercel, Convex, Stripe and Google accounts;
(b) use a **unique, strong password** for every Dono-related account, held in a password manager, and never reuse a personal password;
(c) **never commit a secret, API key, token, credential or connection string to source control**, and tell the incident lead at once if you do;
(d) never store production credentials in plain text, in a note, in a chat message or in a document;
(e) keep any device you use for Dono work **encrypted, password- or biometric-locked, screen-locked when unattended, and running supported, updated software**;
(f) not use public or untrusted Wi-Fi for administrative access without a VPN;
(g) not install unapproved tools, browser extensions or integrations that can reach Dono data;
(h) not share your credentials, or let anyone else use your access;
(i) request access on the **principle of least privilege**, and tell Dono when you no longer need an access you hold; and
(j) report anything that looks like a phishing attempt, a compromise or a vulnerability, immediately.

5.2 Dono does not currently mandate managed devices, formal device management or a security certification, and does not claim to.

## 6. Decision-making authority

6.1 **Amrit Kaur Rooprai is the sole trader and holds final decision authority on everything.** As accountable owner she is also the Online Safety lead, the data-protection lead, the financial-crime lead and the incident lead.

6.2 **You must not, without her express authority:**

(a) change anything affecting payments, fees, refunds or the Stripe configuration;
(b) publish, amend or remove any legal document or public policy;
(c) enable a feature flag for a feature whose Release Control Matrix entry is not `Ready`;
(d) change what personal data is collected, how long it is kept, or where it is processed;
(e) add, change or remove a processor, vendor or third-party integration;
(f) make a referral to an institution, a report to a regulator, or a statement to a regulator, the press or law enforcement;
(g) enter into any contract, or commit Dono to any spend; or
(h) grant anyone access to production systems or personal data.

6.3 **Named deputies** (clause 2 of the Incident Response Plan) may act in her documented absence, within the limits recorded there.

6.4 **Moderation independence.** Where you take a moderation decision, you must not decide an appeal against your own decision (Community Guidelines clause 8.5).

## 7. Conflicts of interest

7.1 You must declare, in writing and promptly, any personal connection to a Campaign Owner, a Society, an Institution or a user whose matter you are handling; any donation you have made to, or promotion you have given, a campaign you are reviewing; and any interest in a competing platform.

7.2 Where a conflict exists you must recuse yourself and hand the matter over. The recusal is recorded with the case.

## 8. Exit and offboarding

8.1 Either party may end this arrangement at any time, on written notice, unless a separate written agreement says otherwise. Clauses 2, 3, 4, 8 and 9 survive.

8.2 **On the day you stop contributing — or immediately, where Dono ends the arrangement for cause — Dono will remove your access to:** production systems and admin tooling; source-code repositories; Vercel; Convex; Stripe; Resend; PostHog; the Google Workspace or Gmail account and the support inbox; the domain registrar; the password manager and every shared credential; the incident communication channel; and any other Dono account or integration.

8.3 **Target: all access removed within 24 hours of your last day, and immediately where the departure is for cause or is contentious.** Dono maintains an access inventory listing every system, its owner and who holds access, so that offboarding is a checklist rather than a memory exercise. **The completed offboarding checklist is signed, dated and retained.**

8.4 **You must, on or before your last day:** return or securely delete every copy of Dono material, code and personal data on any device or account of yours, and confirm in writing that you have; hand over documentation, credentials you hold, and anything in progress; and identify anything of Dono's that remains anywhere outside Dono's systems.

8.5 **Secrets rotation.** Where you held production credentials or shared secrets, Dono rotates those secrets on your departure regardless of the circumstances.

8.6 **Nothing in this clause affects the IP assignment in clause 2 or the confidentiality obligations in clause 3**, both of which continue.

## 8A. Why this agreement is urgent — the verified position

Recorded here because anyone signing this should understand why they are being asked to.

- **There is no code-review gate.** No continuous-integration configuration, no code-owners file and no branch protection exists anywhere in the project. **Anyone with commit access can change the legal text, a document-version constant, the fee calculation or a feature flag and ship it** — and bumping a version constant invalidates every prior acceptance and forces every user to re-accept. That is a one-line change with contractual effect, gated by nothing. Clause 6.2 of this agreement is currently the **only** control over that, which is why it matters that it is signed.
- **Ownership is concentrated in people who have not signed anything.** The legally accountable person has written roughly 4% of the code. Clause 2 is what fixes that.
- **No release owner exists**, no version tags have been created, and the project version has never been incremented — so there is currently no state anyone could meaningfully sign off against.
- **Production access is undocumented.** Nobody holds a record of who can reach the database, hosting, payment, email or analytics consoles. Clause 8.3's offboarding checklist depends on that record existing.

These are tracked as items GV-09 to GV-11, SE-01 and SE-10.

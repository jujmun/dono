> **Read clause 2 before anything else.** Handling suspected child sexual abuse material incorrectly is itself a criminal offence. The single most important rule is: **do not download, copy, screenshot, forward or open it locally.**

---

## 1. The legal obligation

1.1 Dono is a **regulated user-to-user service** under the Online Safety Act 2023 once users can publish campaigns, updates or comments.

1.2 **The CSEA reporting duty applies to every regulated user-to-user service, regardless of size, revenue or assessed risk level.** There is no small-provider exemption. It requires a provider that becomes aware of content it has reasonable grounds to infer is CSEA content, present on the service and generated, uploaded or shared in the UK, to **report it to the National Crime Agency** in the required form and within the required time, and to keep the prescribed records.

1.3 **Related duties that bite at the same moment:** the illegal-content duties (proportionate systems to prevent, detect and swiftly remove); the children's safety duties; the record-keeping duties; and the data-protection obligations governing how the material is stored, accessed and deleted.

1.4 **Related criminal law.** Making, possessing or distributing an indecent image of a child is a criminal offence. There is a limited defence for a person who makes an image for the purposes of the prevention, detection or investigation of crime, but it is narrow and does not protect casual handling. **The safe course is always: do not handle the material; preserve it in place; report it.**

## 2. What a moderator must do — and must never do

### 2.1 Never

- **Never download, copy, screenshot, print, forward, email or save** suspected CSEA material.
- **Never open it on a personal device**, or on any device outside the restricted workflow.
- **Never attach it** to a report, an email, a Signal message or a case note.
- **Never share it** with another team member "to check".
- **Never contact the uploader** about it, or tell them a report has been made.
- **Never delete it** before the report is made and the preservation period has run — deleting evidence can destroy an investigation and may itself be an offence.
- **Never investigate the person** yourself, or attempt to identify a child in the material.

### 2.2 Always

1. **Stop looking.** You do not need to confirm it. Reasonable grounds to suspect is enough.
2. **Note the identifiers only** — the URL, the content ID, the campaign ID, the account ID, the date and time you saw it.
3. **Restrict the content in place** using the moderation tool: hide it from public view without deleting it.
4. **Escalate immediately** to the Online Safety lead by phone, and in the Signal incident group — **identifiers only, never the material or a description of the imagery.**
5. **Write down what you did and when.**
6. **Tell someone how you are.** Seeing this material is harmful. Say so; support is expected, not a weakness (clause 8).

## 3. Preservation

3.1 On escalation, the Online Safety lead or deputy places the material in **restricted storage**: a segregated location, encrypted at rest, accessible only to the Organisation Administrator and Deputy Organisation Administrator, with **every access logged** (who, when, why).

3.2 Preserved alongside the material: the content identifier and URL; the uploading account and its registration data; upload timestamps and IP address; the campaign or comment context; the report or detection route; every moderation action and its time; and the identity of everyone who accessed it.

3.3 **The material is not viewed again** except where strictly necessary to complete the report, and only by an authorised reporter.

3.4 The account is suspended and any other content it has uploaded is restricted pending review.

## 4. Reporting to the NCA

4.1 **Only an authorised reporter may submit a report.** Dono's authorised reporters are the **Organisation Administrator (Amrit Kaur Rooprai)** and the **Deputy Organisation Administrator (Sashank)**. No one else may submit, and no one else has portal credentials.

4.2 **The report is submitted through Dono's registered account on the NCA's CSEA reporting portal**, using the working account credentials established at registration. It must be submitted **without delay** and in any event as soon as reasonably practicable after Dono becomes aware.

4.3 The report includes the information the reporting form prescribes — typically the material or its secure reference; the URL and content identifiers; uploader account and registration details; timestamps; IP address; the detection route; and Dono's contact details.

4.4 **The NCA reference number is recorded immediately** on receipt, in the incident record and the case record.

4.5 **Tipping off.** The uploader is **not** told that a report has been made to the NCA. Where the account is suspended, the notice states that content was removed for a serious breach of the Terms and gives no further detail. This is an express exception to the ordinary notification rule in clause 23.5 of the Terms of Service and clause 8.2 of the Institutional Referral Protocol.

4.6 If law enforcement makes contact, all instructions are followed and nothing is deleted until they confirm it may be.

## 5. Retention and deletion — two separate clocks

| What | Period | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The NCA report reference and the fact of the report** | **5 years** from submission | Restricted storage | Record-keeping duty; ability to respond to law-enforcement follow-up |
| **The reported content and the prescribed supporting information** | **1 year** from submission | Restricted storage, encrypted, access-logged | Preservation for investigation, then deletion to minimise holding unlawful material |

5.1 **These are two clocks, not one.** No document may describe a single five-year period for both.

5.2 **At the end of the 1-year period the content is deleted automatically**, and the deletion is logged with the date, the method and the person who confirmed it. **Deletion is suspended** where the NCA, the police or a court has directed retention, or where a live investigation or legal proceeding requires it; the suspension and the reason are recorded and reviewed every 90 days.

5.3 The 5-year record contains **no imagery** — only the reference, dates, identifiers, the actions taken and the access log.

5.4 **Backups.** Restricted storage is excluded from ordinary backups where technically possible; where it is not, deletion propagation through the 30–35 day backup cycle is confirmed and recorded.

## 6. Data-protection position

6.1 The material is **criminal-offence data** and may be **special category data**. The Article 6 basis is **legal obligation**; the Article 10 / Schedule 1 condition is the condition for **preventing or detecting unlawful acts**, applied as recorded in the Appropriate Policy Document.

6.2 Data-subject rights are restricted here: the exemptions for the prevention and detection of crime and for the protection of individuals apply to access, erasure and notification. **The reasoning for each restriction is recorded case by case**, not assumed.

6.3 The Privacy Notice states the retention clocks in clause 7.1; the ROPA records the activity; the DPIA records the risk.

## 8. Moderator wellbeing

8.1 Encountering this material causes real harm. Dono's commitments: a moderator may stop at any point and hand over; no one is required to view material to "confirm" it; the Online Safety lead checks in with anyone exposed, the same day and again within a week; and no one is criticised for escalating something that turns out to be innocuous.

8.2 Support routes are included in training. **Escalating early is always the right call.**

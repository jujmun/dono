# Dono Institutional Referral Protocol

**Version:** 2.3
**Version date:** 6 August 2026
**Document type:** Internal protocol
**Owner:** Amrit Kaur Rooprai. Deputy: Sashank.
**Resolves review finding:** F60
**Referenced by:** Terms of Service clauses 21.3, 23.7 and 24.2; Privacy Notice clause 9; Refund and Dispute Policy clause 7.1; Student and Society Campaign Terms

> **This protocol is the single source of the rules for referring a matter about a user to their university, college or students' union.** Where any other Dono document mentions an institutional referral, it means this protocol. No referral may be made outside it.

---

## 1. Why this protocol exists

1.1 A referral to an Institution can affect a student's education, reputation, disciplinary record and future. It frequently involves an **allegation** rather than a proven fact, and may involve special category or criminal-offence data. Over-referral, inaccurate wording or disclosing more than is necessary creates data-protection, defamation and fairness exposure — for the student most of all.

1.2 The default is **no referral**. A referral is an exception that must be justified, approved, minimised, documented and — normally — disclosed to the person concerned.

## 2. When a referral may be made

2.1 A referral may be made **only** where all four of the following are satisfied:

(a) **A permitted circumstance applies** (clause 2.2);
(b) **the seriousness threshold is met** (clause 3);
(c) **the necessity test is satisfied** (clause 3); and
(d) **an authorised decision-maker has approved it** (clause 4).

2.2 **Permitted circumstances — exhaustive.**

| # | Circumstance | Typical example |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Safeguarding** — a documented concern that a person is at risk of serious harm, and the Institution is better placed than Dono to act | A campaign or comment disclosing a credible risk of serious self-harm by a student |
| R2 | **Serious fraud or dishonesty** on the Platform by a student or society officer | A fabricated campaign, falsified evidence, or misappropriated campaign funds |
| R3 | **Serious and unremedied breach of the Terms** where Dono's own sanctions have been exhausted or are inadequate | Refusal to refund donors after a determination, following suspension and removal |
| R4 | **Eligibility verification**, where Dono has a **specific reason to doubt** that a person is who or what they claim | A campaign in the name of a society that a reported concern suggests does not exist |
| R5 | **Legal obligation or lawful request** from the Institution acting under a statutory power, a court order or a law-enforcement process | A court order |
| R6 | **The user asks Dono to, or gives consent** | A student asks Dono to confirm to their college that a campaign is genuine |

2.3 **Never a ground for referral:** a campaign that simply failed; a disagreement about a moderation decision; a refund refused on the merits; a single unproven complaint from another user; a matter Dono can resolve itself with its own sanctions; embarrassment; or a desire to demonstrate diligence to an institution.

## 3. Seriousness and necessity test

3.1 The decision-maker must record a written answer to each of the following. **A referral is not permitted unless every answer supports it.**

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| **Seriousness** | Is the matter serious enough that a reasonable person would expect an institution to be told? Would Dono still refer if the student were a friend? |
| **Evidence** | What is the evidence? Is it documented? What is unproven? |
| **Necessity** | Is the referral necessary to achieve a legitimate aim, or merely useful? What would happen if Dono did not refer? |
| **Alternatives** | Has every less intrusive option been considered — a warning, suspension, removal, refund enforcement, a report to the police instead, or telling the user to speak to their institution themselves? Why is each inadequate? |
| **Proportionality** | Is the likely impact on the student proportionate to the aim? Consider disciplinary consequences, visa or funding implications and reputational harm |
| **Recipient** | Is the proposed recipient the correct person, in the correct role, at the correct institution? |
| **Lawful basis** | Which UK GDPR Article 6 basis applies, and — where special category or criminal-offence data is involved — which Article 9 or 10 condition and which Schedule 1 condition applies under the Appropriate Policy Document? |

3.2 **Urgent safeguarding.** Where there is a credible and immediate risk to life or of serious harm, the referral may be made **before** the full record is completed, by the Online Safety lead or a deputy. The record must be completed **within 24 hours**. Where the risk is immediate, the emergency services are the first call, not the Institution.

## 4. Who may approve

4.1 **Only Amrit Kaur Rooprai may approve a referral.** In her absence, **Sashank** may approve it; if neither is available and the matter is urgent, **Joe** may approve an urgent safeguarding referral only.

4.2 **The person who investigated the matter may not be the sole approver** where they are not one of the authorised approvers. No reviewer may approve their own referral without a second authorised approver where one is reasonably available.

4.3 The approval is recorded with the approver's name, the date, the time and the reasoning.

## 5. What may be disclosed

5.1 **The minimum necessary, and nothing more.** The starting point is that Dono discloses only what the Institution needs in order to act.

5.2 **Ordinarily disclosable:**

- the student's name and university email address;
- the campaign identifier and public URL;
- a factual description of the conduct or concern;
- the dates concerned;
- what Dono has done (suspension, removal, refund determination) and why;
- whether the matter is proven, admitted or alleged; and
- what Dono is asking the Institution to do, or that it is for information only.

5.3 **Never disclosed unless specifically necessary and separately justified in the record:**

- a Donor's identity or contact details;
- donation amounts attributable to identified donors;
- the content of unrelated campaigns, comments or correspondence;
- special category data, unless it is the substance of a safeguarding concern and a Schedule 1 condition applies;
- Dono's internal risk scores or unrelated moderation history; or
- payment or Connected Account details.

5.4 **Dono no longer holds student-card numbers or student-card images**, so they can never form part of a referral. Any legacy reference to disclosing a student number to an institution is superseded by this protocol.

## 6. How an allegation must be described

6.1 **Anything not established is described as an allegation, in terms.** The referral must use language such as *"It is alleged that…"*, *"A report has been received stating that…"*, or *"Dono has not established whether…"*.

6.2 The referral must state expressly: **what Dono has established; what remains unproven; what the user has said in response (or that they have not responded); and that Dono is not making a finding of misconduct.**

6.3 A referral must never state or imply that a criminal offence has been committed unless a court has so found.

## 7. Secure communication

7.1 A referral is sent **only** to a named individual in a named role at the Institution, whose role has been confirmed, using an institutional email address at the Institution's own domain.

7.2 Referrals are not sent to generic inboxes, shared addresses, personal addresses, or any address Dono has not verified. Attachments are limited to what clause 5 permits.

7.3 The referral states that it contains personal data, that Dono discloses it as a controller for the purpose stated, that the Institution becomes an independent controller on receipt, and that it must be used only for that purpose.

## 8. Telling the user

8.1 **The default is that the user is told**, at the time of the referral or as soon as reasonably practicable afterwards, and is given: the fact of the referral; the recipient's role and institution; the reason; a summary of what was disclosed; the fact that anything unproven was described as an allegation; and how to seek correction or appeal.

8.2 **Notice may be withheld or delayed only where** telling the user would: prejudice an ongoing investigation by Dono, the Institution, the police or another authority; put a person at risk of harm; breach a legal obligation or a court order; or prejudice the prevention or detection of crime.

8.3 Where notice is withheld, the decision-maker records **which exception applies, why, and when the position will be reviewed.** The position is reviewed at least every **30 days**, and the user is told as soon as the exception no longer applies.

## 9. Correction and appeal

9.1 A user who is the subject of a referral may:

(a) **request correction** of a factual inaccuracy in what was disclosed, at any time and with no deadline; and
(b) **appeal** the underlying moderation, suspension or refund decision under clause 8 of the Community Guidelines, within ten Working Days of being notified.

9.2 **Where a correction is accepted, or an appeal succeeds, Dono must send a correction to the Institution** — to the same recipient, promptly, stating clearly what was wrong, what the correct position is, and that the earlier communication should be disregarded to that extent. The correction is recorded with the original referral.

9.3 A referral is never treated as final while an appeal is outstanding. Where an appeal is pending, the referral must say so.

## 10. Records and audit

10.1 **Every referral is recorded**, whether or not it proceeds, with: the date; the circumstance relied on (R1–R6); the seriousness and necessity answers under clause 3; the alternatives considered and rejected; the lawful basis and any Schedule 1 condition; the approver and the time of approval; the exact information disclosed; the recipient's name, role and institution; the method of transmission; whether the user was told and when, or which exception was applied; any correction sent; and the outcome.

10.2 Referral records are retained for **six years** and are held in restricted storage with logged access.

10.3 The financial-crime and online-safety leads review all referral records **every six months** to check for over-referral, inconsistency, and any pattern suggesting the threshold is being applied too readily. The review is documented.

10.4 Dono publishes no referral statistics that could identify an individual.

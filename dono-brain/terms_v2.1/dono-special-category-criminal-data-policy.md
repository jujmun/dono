# Special Category & Criminal Data Policy

**Dono** | Last updated: [date] | Owner: Amrit
**Status:** Internal record — this document is intended to operate as Dono's Appropriate Policy Document under Part 4 of Schedule 1, Data Protection Act 2018. **To be reviewed by a solicitor before reliance.**

## 1. Purpose

This policy records the categories of special category data (Article 9 UK GDPR) and criminal offence data (Article 10 UK GDPR) that Dono may process, the conditions relied on to process each category, and the safeguards Dono applies. It exists so that, if asked, Dono can demonstrate a documented basis for this processing rather than an ad hoc one.

Dono does not ask users to provide special category or criminal offence data. Where it arises, it does so because a user has chosen to disclose it, or because Dono needs to act on a safeguarding, fraud, or legal concern.

## 2. Where this data arises

**Special category data:**
- Campaign descriptions and comments, where a user voluntarily discloses information revealing health, disability, religion, sexual orientation, or similar characteristics (e.g. a campaign explaining a medical need)
- Donation history, where a pattern of donations could reveal or imply a characteristic (e.g. donations concentrated on LGBTQ+-focused campaigns)

**Criminal offence data:**
- Reports of suspected child sexual exploitation or abuse (CSEA), escalated to the NCA under Dono's Online Safety Act process
- Financial-crime escalations (suspected fraud or money laundering) under Dono's Financial Crime & Payments Policy
- Reports from one user about another alleging criminal conduct

## 3. Conditions relied on — special category data (Article 9)

**Voluntary disclosure in public campaign/profile content:** Dono relies on **Article 9(2)(e)** — data manifestly made public by the data subject — where a user has chosen to include the information in a publicly visible campaign, comment, or profile. Users are told not to include special category data unless it is genuinely necessary for their campaign.

**Fraud prevention, safeguarding, or legal claims:** where special category data needs to be processed for one of these purposes rather than because a user chose to disclose it, Dono relies on the corresponding Schedule 1, Part 2 condition — in practice, **paragraph 10 (preventing or detecting unlawful acts)** for fraud and safeguarding concerns, or **Article 9(2)(f)** where processing is necessary for the establishment, exercise, or defence of legal claims.

**Open issue for solicitor review:** donation history is not itself disclosed by the user as special category data — it is *inferred* from a pattern of otherwise-public donations. Article 9(2)(e) is a weaker fit here, since the inference is Dono's, not the user's own disclosure. Until this is confirmed, Dono should avoid actively deriving or labelling special category inferences from donation history (e.g. no internal tagging of users by inferred characteristics), and should treat any such inference as data to be minimised rather than used.

## 4. Conditions relied on — criminal offence data (Article 10)

All three sources in Section 2 rely on **Schedule 1, Part 2, paragraph 10 (preventing or detecting unlawful acts)**, on the basis that:
- the processing is necessary for that purpose, and
- asking for the data subject's consent would prejudice the purpose (a suspected offender cannot be asked to consent to being reported).

For CSEA reports specifically, this sits alongside Dono's statutory obligations and Amrit's designated role under the Online Safety Act. For user-to-user reports, Dono only processes the allegation itself (not further criminal record data), and treats unresolved reports as allegations, not findings.

## 5. Data protection principles

- **Minimisation:** Dono does not seek out or require special category or criminal offence data. Where it appears in campaign or comment content, it is retained only as part of that content, not extracted or separately tagged.
- **Security:** access to escalated financial-crime and CSEA records is limited to Amrit and staff Amrit directs, consistent with the access controls in Dono's other compliance policies.
- **Retention:** special category data within campaign/comment content is retained for as long as the campaign or comment remains live, per Dono's general content retention approach. Criminal offence data arising from escalations is retained in the incident log described in Dono's Financial Crime & Payments Policy and Online Safety Act process, for as long as needed to respond to the concern and for a reasonable period after in case of follow-up from Stripe, the NCA, or law enforcement.

## 6. Governance and review

Amrit is accountable for this policy, consistent with Amrit's designated roles for Online Safety Act reporting and financial-crime escalation. This document should be reviewed alongside those two policies, and whenever Dono's product features change in a way that could introduce new special category or criminal offence data (e.g. new profile fields, new campaign categories).

This document, and the incident-level records referenced in it, will be retained for as long as the relevant processing continues, plus a minimum of six months afterwards, and made available to the ICO on request.

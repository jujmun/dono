# Dono legal suite — v2.1 reconciliation report (Step 1)

**Date:** 31 July 2026
**Purpose:** cross-check of `terms_v2.1/` (9 public documents + 15 internal documents) against the pre-launch counsel review (30 July 2026) and the founder's amendments, plus the two engineering documents (`developer-configuration-form.md`, `cookie-configuration-review.md`) and `engineering/terms-engineering-questionnaire-answers.md`.
**Status:** issue list only. No document has been changed. `terms_v2.1/` is untouched.

## How the documents depend on each other

The suite is more tightly coupled than its file structure suggests. The dependency chains that matter:

**Chain 1 — payment architecture.** `ToS 15` (Recipient model) → `ToS 16` (fees) → `Refund 6, 8, 9` (execution, chargebacks, fee treatment) → `Donor 6, 12` → `Student 9` → `Society 4, 7` → `Financial Crime Policy 5` → `Illegal-Content RA 2` → `Children's RA 1, 6`. Nine documents restate the same three factual claims: Dono never holds funds, Dono cannot initiate a refund, and Dono can withhold a payout. Change any one and all nine move.

**Chain 2 — age and children.** `ToS 1.8` (18+) → `Donor 2.1` → `Verification 4.3` → `Privacy 14.2` → `Online Safety Procedures 3.3` (children's access assessment) → `Children's RA 2` → `Illegal-Content RA 2A` (which re-rates CSEA, harassment and intimate-image abuse on the answer). The internal documents assume a different age position from the public ones.

**Chain 3 — society capacity.** `ToS 3.1` (definitions of Society, Responsible Individual, Recipient) → `Society 1–3, 6` → `Society Forms 8.1, 8.2` → `Verification 5.2` → `Student 1.3, 10.3` → `Refund 7`. The Society Terms define who the refund debtor is; the Refund Policy assumes that person exists and can be identified.

**Chain 4 — data protection.** `Privacy 4` (lawful basis table) → `ROPA` (rows 1–12) → `LIA 1–4` → `DPIA` → `APD` → `DPA Register` → `International Transfer Assessment` → `Cookie Notice 3, 5`. The ROPA is meant to be the source of truth for the other six; it currently disagrees with all of them.

**Chain 5 — complaints and appeals.** `ToS 23.5, 33` → `Community 8.2` → `Refund 5.6` → `Donor 11.6` → `Complaints Policy 3, 5, 6` → `Online Safety Procedures 3.5` → `DP Complaints Workflow` → `Verification 9.1`. Eight documents describe overlapping processes with four different clocks, two different email addresses and three different rules on who reviews an appeal.

**Chain 6 — definitions that leak.** `ToS 3.1` is the definitions clause for the whole contractual suite, but four terms used across the internal documents are not defined there at all: **Beneficiary**, **institution verification**, **asset owner** and **Authorised Society Representative**.

# BLOCKING

Items that would make a v2.2 document untrue, unenforceable, or internally contradictory if left as they are.

## A. Age, children and the Online Safety Act

**1. Whether a donor must be 18 is answered three different ways.**
`ToS 1.8` and `Donor 2.1` say you must be at least 18 to make a Donation. Your amendment says "Donors may be under 18 unless Dono introduces an age restriction for donating." `Children's RA §2` says donors can be any age and that "guest checkout requires no account and no age check". Engineering (`questionnaire B3`) says an 18+ self-declared DOB gate **is** enforced on donate (`assertDonateGates` / `DonateDobGate`) and `ageAttested` is stored on the donation row. Three of the four sources are wrong about the product as built.

**2. Whether a commenter is age- or identity-verified is answered three different ways.**
`ToS 1.8` requires 18+ to create an account, with no verification. `Children's RA §1 and §6` state that Stripe Identity verification applies "for all campaign creators **and commenters**". `Illegal-Content RA 2A` states that "a person under 18 could hold an account, view campaigns, donate, comment publicly and hold a profile with a photograph". Engineering: Stripe Identity is required for campaign and society **creation** only.

**3. The children's risk assessment rests on a false premise about age assurance.**
`Children's RA` opens by arguing Dono's position is stronger than a Terms clause "because publishing is gated by an actual verification mechanism (Stripe Identity), not a checkbox". Engineering B3 says the opposite: Stripe Identity's `verifiedDob` is "**not guaranteed** for every document/path" and is "**not used for age enforcement**" — the gate is a self-declared date of birth. Under Ofcom's children's access assessment tool, self-declaration is not highly effective age assurance (review C06). The assessment's Low ratings inherit this error.

**4. The age question is recorded as both decided and open.**
`Online Safety Procedures 3.3` and `Children's RA` record a decision ("child-safe by default, rather than deploying age assurance"). `Illegal-Content RA 2A` records it as "the single most consequential item for you to resolve before launch" and rates CSEA (grooming) **Medium** "pending resolution of Section 2A", with harassment and intimate-image ratings also contingent. Both cannot stand.

**5. Schools are in scope in two internal documents and out of scope in the Terms.**
`Children's RA §1` says beneficiaries are "verified as affiliated with the university **or school** named in the campaign". `Illegal-Content RA 2A` refers to "school-age fundraising beneficiaries' friends". `ToS 3.1` and `4.4` limit participation to UK **higher-education** institutions. If schools are genuinely in scope, under-18 beneficiaries are in scope and the entire children's analysis changes.

## B. Payments, fees and refunds — the Terms versus the code

**6. "Dono does not initiate refunds" is contradicted by the live code.**
`ToS 15.4`, `Refund 6.1`, `Donor 12.1`, `Student 9.2` and `Society 4.2` all state categorically that Dono does not initiate refunds and "holds no reserve power to do so". Your amendment repeats it ("we cannot hold payouts or initiate refunds"). Engineering `F1`: a Dono admin approves a `refundRequest` and `processApprovedRefund` calls `stripe.refunds.create` with `stripeAccount` set to the connected account — a **platform-initiated refund on the owner's connected account**, already built. Engineering's own note: "code ≠ 'owner-only' Terms — soften Terms or change code before publication." Review C03 and H06 predicted this exactly.

**7. Application-fee refunds are built, and the Terms treat them as unknown.**
`Refund 9.3` flags the two-step mechanics as `[CONFIRM WITH STRIPE]`. Engineering `F2` / `1.6` / `1.7`: built and webhook-driven — on `charge.refunded` or a lost dispute, `stripe.applicationFees.createRefund` reverses the proportional delta, tracked as `applicationFeeRefundedMinor`. It is proportional on partial refunds.

**8. The power to withhold payouts does not exist and is claimed in six places.**
`Refund 7.1`, `Student 9.5`, `Society 7.1`, `Donor 12.2` and `Financial Crime Policy 5` all reserve a right to "withhold future payouts" or "ask Stripe to hold the associated payout". `Children's RA §1` and `§6` list "ask Stripe to hold payouts" as a **confirmed existing safety measure**. Engineering `1.9` / `F3`: not built, and "payments architecture forbids payout holds (FCA perimeter)"; with `losses_collector: "stripe"` the platform cannot pause connected-account payouts at all.

**9. The fee model in the Terms is not the fee model in the code.**
`ToS 16.1` and `Donor 6.1` describe "the Payment Provider's applicable fee for the transaction **plus 3.5 percentage points**". The code implements a **fixed 5% + 20p envelope** (`calculateFeeEnvelopeMinor`): the estimated Stripe UK standard fee (1.5% + 20p) is allocated from the envelope first and Dono's `application_fee_amount` is the **residual**. These produce the same answer only for a standard UK card. For premium, EEA, international and FX transactions the Terms overstate what the donor is charged and understate what the connected account absorbs.

**10. The published card-rate table is factually wrong.**
`ToS 16.2` states premium UK 1.9% + 20p and international 3.25% + 20p. Stripe's current published UK pricing (fetched 31 July 2026, dev form §2) is **2.8% + 20p** and **3.15% + 20p**. `Donor 6.1` repeats the wrong figures.

**11. The "exact total at checkout" promise cannot be met.**
`ToS 16.5` and `Donor 6.4` promise the exact total "computed from the card category once known". Engineering `2.2`: the card category is **not** known before the final charge; checkout always estimates UK standard, and actual fees settle later on the connected account. `ToS 16.5` has a fallback ("method and maximum") but the envelope is not a true maximum by card type.

**12. Recurring donations are a prohibited category and a shipped feature.**
`ToS 8.4` lists "recurring donations" among prohibited categories. Engineering `C1`, `1.5`, `C2`: monthly donations run as Stripe Subscriptions with `application_fee_percent`, and `societySubscription*` fan-out is live.

**13. Matching funds are a prohibited category and a shipped feature.**
`ToS 8.4` prohibits "matching funds". Engineering `C2`: donations carry `matchedAmountPounds` and `matchWindowId`.

**14. "Dono never holds funds" is untrue on one payment path, and two engineering documents disagree about whether that path exists.**
`ToS 4.2`, `15.3`, `Donor 1.2` and `Refund 1.1` all state flatly that Dono never receives, holds or controls donation funds. `ToS 8.4` separately prohibits "pooled funds". Engineering `1.1` and `3.1`: "**community-fund gifts are charged on the platform account (platform is MoR for that path)**". But `engineering/payments-architecture.md` line 35 states "No escrow, no pooled funds, **no community funds**, no virtual cards, no reallocating money between parties." This is the sentence the FCA perimeter conclusion (review C10) turns on.

**15. The subscription path applies a different fee mechanic from the one-time path.**
Engineering `1.5`: monthly subscriptions set `application_fee_percent: 5%` raw — the Stripe-share residual split is not applied. Engineering flags this itself as an inconsistency. The Terms describe only one fee mechanic.

**16. Loss, fee and dispute allocation is blank in the Terms and answered in the code.**
`ToS 15.5` is an empty `[CONFIRM WITH STRIPE]`. Engineering: losses collector = **Stripe**; fees collector = connected account (UK dispute fee **£20**); dispute owner = **connected account**. Separately, `Refund 8.2` promises "Dono assembles and submits the defence evidence on the Campaign Owner's behalf" — engineering `1.4`: there is **no dispute-evidence submission API**, only `charge.dispute.created` / `closed` webhooks, and who is obliged to submit evidence is an open decision.

**17. The de minimis threshold is in four documents and removed by your amendment, but the reasoning doesn't fully carry.**
`ToS 14.5`, `Student 6.1`, `Donor 10.3` and `Refund 10.3` all carry a `[TO BE CALCULATED]` de minimis. Your amendment: "Reverse chronological with **no de minimis** for refunds; the campaign won't let you donate more than the campaign amount." Capping donations at the target prevents *over-funding* surplus, but the documents treat the main source of surplus as **under-spend** (`ToS 14.5`, `Refund 10.2` — "any amount that cannot reasonably be used for the stated purpose"). Under-spend surplus can still produce sub-£1 refunds.

## C. Legal entity, society capacity and succession

**18. Two legal identities run through the suite.**
Public documents correctly name Amrit Kaur Rooprai trading as Dono. `ROPA` header names "Dono (UK Ltd, incorporation pending)". `Illegal-Content RA` document control names "Dono (UK Ltd — incorporation not yet started)" and `§8.1` commits Amrit to reporting "to the company's most senior governing body **once incorporated**". **19. Your amendments disagree with each other about the secondary society representative.**
Your amendment says: "I would remove the secondary representative **entirely**. Every campaign has exactly one: Authorised Society Representative." A later note in the same document says: "lets just have a **backup** in the society that can take ownership". `Society 6.1` requires that every Society Campaign "must **always** have a primary Society Representative **and** a secondary Responsible Individual". `Society Forms 8.2` captures both at setup.

**20. Four different answers to who holds the society's Stripe account.**
`Society 3.1` offers four options (society account / college or university account / registered charity / authorised representative's personal account). Your amendment: "Students use their **personal account** but it is fundamentally linked to the society... it's the same Stripe account, just a different name on the legal stuff." Engineering `3.1`: society campaigns actually use a **society-held connected account keyed by `communitySlug`, shared by society leaders** — "not college/university or charity by default". Review H23: do not permit personal-account society campaigns at beta.

**21. The mandatory balance transfer on succession is impossible.**
`Society 6.4` and `Society Forms 8.2 step 4` say a remaining balance "**must be transferred** to the incoming Representative's new Connected Account, or to the Society/institution account, before the old account is disconnected". Engineering `3.3`: "**No in-product balance migration.** Creating a new Connect account does not auto-move balances" — it requires a payout plus an external transfer. Review H23 flags this as a commingling, tax and authority risk.

**22. Registered charities are prohibited and permitted in the same suite.**
`ToS 8.4` prohibits "fundraising for a registered charity". `ToS 15.1` and `Society 3.1` list "a registered charity" as a permitted Recipient. Review M18. Unresolved in v2.1.

**23. "Recognised Institutions" is plural in the Terms and singular in the code.**
`ToS 3.1` and `4.4` describe a general class of recognised UK institutions. Engineering `B4`: sign-up is restricted to the `ox.ac.uk` email domain. At launch the service is Oxford-only.

## D. Definitions that leak across documents

**24. "Beneficiary" is used in four incompatible senses and is not defined anywhere.**
`ToS 3.1` defines Campaign Owner, Recipient, Society, Student — but **not Beneficiary**; and `ToS 8.1` / `8.4` prohibit "fundraising on behalf of another individual", which implies beneficiary and Campaign Owner are the same person. Against that:
- `Illegal-Content RA §2`: "**Beneficiaries are UK-based only**" and `§8.2` requires "a clear and lawful beneficiary" — a separate person from the creator.
- `Children's RA §1`: "Beneficiaries: verified as affiliated with the university or school named in the campaign" — a verified third party.
- `ROPA row 3`: data subjects are "Donors, **beneficiaries**" — a data-subject category.
- `DP Complaints Form`: offers "**Beneficiary**" as a selectable user type.
- Your amendment's financial-crime table: prohibits "**anonymous beneficiaries**" and makes "beneficiary outside the UK" an enhanced-review trigger.
- `ToS 14.4(c)`: "changing the **beneficiary**" is a fundamental Material Change.

**25. "Institution verification" is an advertised control that does not exist.**
`Children's RA §1` and `§6` list "Institution verification for beneficiaries (confirms university/school affiliation)" as a **confirmed** existing safety measure, and `Illegal-Content RA §8.2` requires checking "verified student, society or institutional status". The `Verification Notice` describes only a student-card image and university-email check by a Dono administrator. There is no institution-side confirmation step in the product or in any policy.

**26. Campaign Owner, Recipient, asset owner and Connected Account holder are collapsed.**
`Refund 1.1` writes "the Campaign Owner's (the Recipient's) own Stripe connected account", assuming they are always the same. With a society-held shared account (item 20) they are genuinely different people, and `Society 3.3` separately records an "asset owner" who may be a third. `Society Forms 8.1 field 7` then conflates the two by calling the asset-owner field the person who will "hold and receive the funds". Review L02, M19.

## G. Contract formation and acceptance

**53. There is no formation mechanism, and your amendment supplies one.**
`ToS 12.1(a)` and `Donor 3.2`: a contract forms "when the Donation is successfully **processed and accepted for the Campaign**" — "accepted" is undefined and no agency or standing-offer mechanism exists. It also specifies a checkout "You're donating to" panel naming the Campaign, Campaign Owner, verified representative, receiving Stripe account and contracting party. **55. The acceptance record promised in the Terms is broader than the one built.**
`ToS 2.2` promises the record stores "the user, the role, the Campaign where relevant, the document names and versions, the timestamp, and the acceptance wording displayed", plus a durable copy. Engineering `D1`: stores `userId` or `guestKey`, `documentId`, version string, `context` and `acceptedAt` — **not** role, campaign id, wording shown, or IP. `D2`: **no immutable document archive** — old bodies are not preserved when a version bumps. `D3`: **no durable copy** by email or download. Review H24.

## H. Liability, indemnity and consumer law

**56. The £100 / £250 caps are in five places and your amendment removes them.**
`ToS 27.2(b)` £100 for consumers and `27.3(b)` £250 for campaign owners and societies, repeated at `Donor 14.3`, `Student 12.1` and `Society 8.1`. Your amendment: remove the blanket caps for consumer claims, limit liability to **reasonably foreseeable** loss, **exclude indirect losses** (lost profits, opportunity, reputation, business interruption, consequential), keep the statutory carve-outs, and draft a separate regime with a commercially reasonable cap for genuine business users. Review H02 agrees. Note that `ToS 27.7` still says the caps "are expected to increase" as Dono incorporates and obtains insurance — but per item 18 there will be no incorporation, and review L12 records that no insurance is in place.

**57. The indemnity only works once "consumer" is defined.**
`ToS 28.1` exempts "consumers" from any indemnity; `ToS 28.2` imposes an uncapped indemnity on "campaign owners and societies". The suite never says whether an individual student campaign owner is a consumer. Review H28: labelling a student a "campaign owner" does not remove Consumer Rights Act fairness review. The same gap breaks `27.2` versus `27.3`.

**58. The Consumer Contracts Regulations analysis covers only one of two payment structures.**
`Donor 5.2` addresses only the case where the donor covers Dono's fee. Your amendment supplies the two-structure table — donor covers the fee (donor is Dono's customer, CCR applies) versus fee deducted (the **Campaign Owner** is Dono's customer, CCR applies only if they are a consumer) — and asks for an internal CCR matrix treating the donation itself as a third, separate relationship. `ToS 16.4` and `Donor 6.3` present both options without any of this. Review H03.

**59. Deemed acceptance of a Closure Statement is unresolved between the review and your amendments.**
`ToS 10.3(e)` and `Student 7.4` make silence acceptance after 30 days. Review M06 recommends removing deemed substantive acceptance in favour of "administratively closed". Your amendments do not address it. Engineering `E4`: the Closure Statement is **not built** as a structured form with deadlines or deemed acceptance at all.

**60. "Hide my name" promises more than the architecture can deliver.**
`ToS 11.3`, `Donor 8.1` and `Privacy 10.1` promise the name is hidden from the public page **and from the Campaign Owner**. Your amendment: "hide my name means hide the name publicly; **we will always see the name on the payment**". With direct charges on a society-held connected account whose leaders hold the **full Stripe Dashboard** (engineering `1.1`, `1.10`), the recipient's own dashboard shows payer data. Review H05.

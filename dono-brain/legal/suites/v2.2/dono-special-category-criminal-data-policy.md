## Changes in v2.2

- An **Article 6 basis is recorded for every activity**, alongside the Article 9 or Article 10 condition. An Appropriate Policy Document that gives only the special-category condition is incomplete, because the ordinary lawful basis is still required.
- **Specific retention periods** replace "a reasonable period".
- **Procedures are recorded against each of the Article 5 principles**, which is what the ICO's structure requires.
- The open issue about **inferring characteristics from donation history** is closed with a rule: Dono does not do it.
- **Reports to the National Crime Agency** are added as a distinct source, with their own two retention clocks.
- **Accuracy labelling of allegations** is stated as an operating rule.

## 1. Purpose

1.1 This policy records the categories of special category data (Article 9 UK GDPR) and criminal offence data (Article 10 UK GDPR) that Dono may process, the Article 6 basis and the Schedule 1 condition relied on for each, and the safeguards applied. It exists so that Dono can demonstrate a documented basis for this processing rather than an ad hoc one.

1.2 **Dono does not ask users to provide special category or criminal offence data.** Where it arises, it does so because a user chose to disclose it, or because Dono must act on a safeguarding, fraud, online-safety or legal concern.

## 2. Where this data arises

**Special category data:**

- **Campaign descriptions and comments**, where a user voluntarily discloses information revealing health, disability, religion, sexual orientation or a similar characteristic — for example a campaign explaining a medical need or a religious society's activity.
- **Uploaded receipts and evidence**, where a document incidentally reveals such information.
- **Moderation reports**, where a reporter's description of the concern discloses it.

**Criminal offence data:**

- **Reports of suspected child sexual exploitation and abuse (CSEA)**, escalated to the National Crime Agency under the Online Safety Act Procedures.
- **Moderation decisions** recording that content was assessed as, or suspected of being, illegal.
- **Financial-crime escalations** — suspected fraud, money laundering or sanctions evasion — under the Financial Crime & Payments Policy.
- **Reports from one user about another** alleging criminal conduct.
- **Records relating to a legal claim**, where the claim concerns alleged criminal conduct.

**What does not arise.** Dono does not process biometric data for identification, genetic data, or trade-union membership. Dono does not receive government identity documents, so it does not process the special category data those documents can contain.

## 3. Article 6 basis and Article 9 condition, by activity

| Activity | Article 6 basis | Article 9 condition | Necessity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Special category data a user chose to publish in a campaign, comment or profile | Contract (providing the Platform); legitimate interests (moderation) | **Article 9(2)(e)** — manifestly made public by the data subject | Dono cannot host a campaign without processing what the creator wrote in it. Users are told not to include sensitive data unless genuinely necessary for the campaign |
| Special category data in an uploaded receipt | Legitimate interests (verifying use of funds) | **Article 9(2)(f)** — establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims, where the evidence relates to a dispute; otherwise the data is minimised or removed rather than processed | Dono needs the expenditure detail, not the sensitive detail. Redaction guidance at upload and Dono's right to redact are the primary controls |
| Pre-publication moderation of a campaign containing special category data | Legitimate interests (fraud, safeguarding, protecting a mixed-age audience); legal obligation (Online Safety Act duties) | **Article 9(2)(e)** for published material; **Schedule 1, Part 2, paragraph 10** where the review concerns a suspected unlawful act | A human must read the campaign in order to review it |
| Special category data processed for fraud prevention or safeguarding | Legitimate interests; legal obligation | **Schedule 1, Part 2, paragraph 10** — preventing or detecting unlawful acts | Consent would prejudice the purpose; a suspected wrongdoer cannot be asked to consent to being investigated |
| Special category data processed for a legal claim | Legitimate interests; legal obligation | **Article 9(2)(f)** | Dono must be able to defend or bring a claim |

## 4. Article 6 basis and Article 10 condition, by activity

| Activity | Article 6 basis | Article 10 condition | Necessity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reports to the NCA of CSEA content** | **Legal obligation** — Online Safety Act 2023 s.66 and the 2026 Regulations | **Schedule 1, Part 2, paragraph 10** | The report is compulsory. Seeking consent is obviously inappropriate and would prejudice the purpose |
| Moderation decisions recording suspected illegal content | Legal obligation (Online Safety Act duties); legitimate interests | **Schedule 1, Part 2, paragraph 10** | Dono must record why it removed or retained content, both to comply and to allow an appeal |
| Financial-crime escalations | Legitimate interests; legal obligation for sanctions | **Schedule 1, Part 2, paragraph 10** | Consent would prejudice the purpose |
| User-to-user reports alleging criminal conduct | Legal obligation (reporting duties); legitimate interests | **Schedule 1, Part 2, paragraph 10** | Dono cannot operate a reporting duty without processing the allegation |
| Records relating to a legal claim | Legitimate interests | **Schedule 1, Part 2, paragraph 10**; and Article 9(2)(f) where special category data is also involved | Necessary to bring or defend the claim |

**Dono only processes the allegation itself.** It does not seek criminal-record data, does not ask users about convictions, and does not obtain police records.

## 5. Inference — a closed rule

The previous version left open whether Article 9(2)(e) could support **inferring** a characteristic from a pattern of otherwise-public donations — for example inferring religion or sexual orientation from the campaigns a person supports. It cannot: the inference would be Dono's, not the data subject's own disclosure, so Article 9(2)(e) is a poor fit and no other condition obviously applies.

**The rule is therefore:**

**Dono does not derive, tag, record, segment or act on any inference about a person's health, disability, religion, political opinions, sexual orientation, sex life, racial or ethnic origin, or trade-union membership, from their donation history, browsing behaviour or any other pattern of activity.** No internal tagging of users by inferred characteristic. No analytics segment built on one. No use of one in moderation, fraud scoring or marketing.

Where such an inference would arise incidentally, it is treated as data to be minimised, not used. This rule is reflected in the Privacy Notice (clause 12.2) and in the ROPA.

## 6. Procedures against each data-protection principle

**Lawfulness, fairness and transparency.** The Article 6 basis and the Schedule 1 condition for each activity are in sections 3 and 4. The Privacy Notice tells users what Dono processes, why and on what basis, and clause 12 addresses special category and criminal-offence data specifically. Where Dono holds data about someone who is not a user — a person named on a receipt — the Privacy Notice clause 11.3 is publicly available as an Article 14 measure.

**Purpose limitation.** Each category is processed only for the purpose recorded in sections 3 and 4. Moderation data is not used for marketing. Financial-crime records are not used for product analytics. CSEA material is used only for the report and is not accessible for any other purpose.

**Data minimisation.** Dono does not seek out special category or criminal-offence data. Where it appears in campaign or comment content it is retained as part of that content and is **not extracted, tagged or separately indexed**. Redaction guidance is given at the point of receipt upload, and Dono reserves the right to redact. Section 5 prohibits inference.

**Accuracy.** **An unresolved allegation is recorded as an allegation and never as a finding.** Moderation and escalation records must state the status of the matter — reported, under review, upheld, not upheld, referred — and must not be phrased as though guilt were established. Where a decision is reversed on appeal, the record is updated to show that. Where a referral has been made to an institution and the position later changes, Dono corrects the record with the recipient.

**Storage limitation.** Specific periods are in section 7.

**Integrity and confidentiality.** Access to escalated financial-crime and CSEA records is limited to Amrit and those Amrit directs. Every administrator access to identity data is audit-logged. CSEA material is held in restricted storage with the minimum number of people able to reach it, and must never be downloaded to a personal device, screenshotted or forwarded.

**Accountability.** This document, the ROPA and the DPIA are reviewed together, and this document is made available to the ICO on request.

## 7. Retention

| Category | Period | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Special category data within campaign or comment content | As for the content itself — campaign pages remain accessible indefinitely at their direct URL; comments until deleted or removed, with a moderation copy kept **6 years** from removal | Publication / removal |
| Special category data in uploaded receipts and evidence | **6 years** | Campaign completion |
| Moderation decisions and reports | **6 years** | Resolution or the enforcement decision |
| Financial-crime escalation records | **6 years** | Case closure |
| Legal claim records | Duration of the matter + **6 years** | Conclusion |
| **NCA CSEA report reference** | **5 years** | Report submitted |
| **CSEA content, information submitted, the material used to make the CSEA judgement, and associated user data** | **1 year**, in restricted storage, then securely deleted — unless the NCA, the police or another competent authority lawfully requires longer | Report submitted |
| This policy and its version history | For as long as the relevant processing continues, plus **6 years** | — |

The two CSEA clocks are deliberately different and must be implemented as two separate automated deletion dates. **Do not apply a single five-year period to CSEA material** — that would cause excessive retention of the most sensitive and potentially unlawful material Dono will ever hold.

## 8. Individual rights and exemptions

Rights apply as set out in the Privacy Notice, subject to the exemptions in the Data Protection Act 2018. In particular, Dono may withhold information or refuse a request where complying would be likely to prejudice the prevention or detection of crime, the apprehension or prosecution of offenders, or an ongoing safeguarding matter. **Any reliance on an exemption is recorded**, with the reason and who decided it, and is reviewed when the matter concludes.

A person cannot use a subject-access request to obtain the identity of someone who reported them, or the content of a CSEA report.

## 9. Sharing

Special category and criminal-offence data is shared only: with the **National Crime Agency**, under the statutory CSEA reporting duty; with the **police or another competent authority**, where Dono reports a suspected crime or is lawfully required to provide information; with **OFSI**, on a suspected sanctions breach; with a **Recognised Institution**, under the referral protocol in clause 23.7 of the Terms of Service, with the allegation labelled as an allegation; and with **professional advisers**, for a legal claim. Each disclosure is recorded.

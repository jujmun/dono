## Purpose

Refund decisions are made by whichever team member picks up the case, not by a panel. This checklist exists so that different reviewers reach the same decision on the same facts. Work through it in order; do not skip to a conclusion.

**Read it alongside the Refund and Dispute Policy.** Where this checklist and the Policy differ, **the Policy governs** — and tell Amrit, so the checklist can be corrected.

## Step 1 — Conflict check. Do this first.

Before looking at the substance, check whether you should be the one deciding it. Hand the case to another reviewer if any of these apply:

- you know the Campaign Owner or the donor personally;
- you donated to, shared or promoted this campaign yourself;
- you have a personal or financial stake in this campaign's outcome; or
- you made the decision now being challenged (for an appeal).

If any apply, record the recusal — who handed the case to whom, and why — alongside the case record, and do not weigh in informally afterwards.

## Step 2 — Identify every ground engaged

**Identify all grounds that arguably apply, not one.** Record each, and whether it is made out. A request can succeed on any one of them.

> **The rule that governs this whole step.** Under Refund and Dispute Policy clause 3.1, **every ground stands on its own**. If a ground is made out, a refund is available. **Never ask whether the matter was material to the Donor's decision to donate, whether they relied on it, or whether it caused the campaign's outcome.** Those questions have no place in a Dono refund decision. Proportionality is relevant only to **how much** is refunded (Step 4), never to **whether** a refund is available.

### Class A — Objective grounds

**These are established by the fact itself.** Skip to Step 4.

| Ground | Made out when |
|---|---|
| Duplicate payment | The same donor paid the same campaign twice in circumstances indicating one was unintended |
| Payment error | The amount charged differs from the amount confirmed, or a technical fault caused the charge |
| Unauthorised payment | The donor did not authorise the payment. **Tell the donor immediately to contact their card issuer** — their statutory claim is against the issuer and may require a refund by the end of the next business day. Dono's process does not replace or delay that |
| **Donation by a person under 18** | Credible evidence shows the Donor was under 18 when the Donation was made. Parent or guardian permission is not an exception. Seek only proportionate evidence and do not interrogate the family |
| Campaign cancellation | The Campaign was cancelled before the money was spent |
| Verification failure | The Society Representative's institutional email verification or the Payment Provider's identity check failed or was withdrawn, and the Campaign should not have been published |
| Surplus | The amount falls to be refunded as Surplus Funds under clause 10 of the Policy |

### Class B — Conduct grounds

**These depend on what the Campaign Owner did or failed to do. Apply Step 3, then Step 4.**

| Ground | Made out when |
|---|---|
| Material misrepresentation | A stated purpose or fact was untrue |
| Fraud | The campaign or the Campaign Owner's identity was fabricated |
| Misuse of funds | Funds were used for something other than the stated purpose |
| Failure to provide required evidence | Receipts or evidence were not submitted after reminders |
| Unauthorised Material Change | A Material Change was made without notification or approval, or was made after funds were spent in order to regularise that spending |
| Inability to use the money meaningfully | The money can no longer be used for the stated purpose |
| Failure to proceed | The Project did not proceed and funds should have been returned |
| No Closure Statement | A Closure Statement was not published after reminders |

## Step 3 — Balance of probabilities (Class B only)

Is it more likely than not that the ground occurred? This is not a criminal standard: you do not need certainty and do not need to rule out every alternative explanation. Base the decision on the evidence actually available — campaign content as published, updates, evidence submitted, Stripe records, and any response from the Campaign Owner — not on assumption.

**Do not draw an adverse inference from silence unless the Campaign Owner received the notice and had a fair opportunity to respond** (Policy clause 5.4: ten Working Days, extendable on reasonable request).

## Step 4 — Decide the remedy

**The only question at this step is *how much*, never *whether*.**

- **No refund** — no ground made out.
- **Full refund** — the ground affects the whole donation.
- **Partial refund** — the ground affects part of it. **The refund is the proportion of the donation attributable to the affected part**: if a campaign raised money for three items and one was misrepresented, the refund is the proportion attributable to that item. Record the arithmetic.
- **Apportionment** — where responsibility is shared, reduce proportionately and record why.

> **Misuse of funds has no de minimis.** Where money was applied to something **outside the stated purpose, or to a purpose not strongly related to it, the whole of the affected amount is refundable.** There is no allowance for small deviations and no threshold below which a misapplication is overlooked (Policy clause 4.3).

Also decide: **does Dono refund its own platform fee?** Yes wherever the donor was not at fault — proportionately on a partial refund. No where the donor caused the refund through their own error. Any fee-cover amount the donor paid is treated the same way as the fee (Policy clause 9.2).

## Step 5 — Warn about external deadlines

**Every outcome letter must tell both parties that external deadlines run independently of Dono's process** — card-scheme chargeback windows and statutory limitation periods do not pause while Dono decides, and Dono's 21-day target may consume part of the donor's chargeback window. Say this even where the refund is granted.

## Step 6 — Record the decision

For every decision, record:

- every ground considered, and whether each was made out;
- for Class B grounds, the balance-of-probabilities reasoning;
- any conflict or recusal note;
- the remedy and, for a partial refund, the arithmetic;
- the fee-refund decision;
- the outcome and, if refused, the reason given to the donor;
- confirmation that the external-deadline warning was given; and
- the appeal deadline (ten Working Days **from notification**) and how to appeal.

Both parties receive the outcome and reasons.

## Step 7 — Pre-execution checks. All four are mandatory.

**No refund is executed until every one of these is confirmed and recorded.**

| # | Check | Why |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | The determination is **recorded** under Step 6 | An unrecorded refund cannot be justified, appealed or audited |
| (b) | The Connected Account holder was **notified and given the opportunity to respond** — **except** in urgent fraud, unauthorised-payment or harm cases, where the reason for proceeding without notice is recorded instead | Fairness, and the condition on which the refund mandate is justified (LIA 8) |
| (c) | The transaction's **dispute state** is checked, and **no chargeback is open or already resolved in the Donor's favour** | Prevents double recovery and a wasted dispute fee |
| (d) | The **amount, the authorising person and the time** are recorded | Accountability for a movement of someone else's money |

## Step 8 — Execute, and record which route was used

The refund is executed by **one** of two routes. Record which:

1. **By the Recipient**, from their own Connected Account; or
2. **By Dono**, instructing the Payment Provider to reverse the charge on the Connected Account **under the mandate in Terms of Service clause 13.2**.

Record in both cases: the route used, the **Payment Provider's refund reference**, the date, the amount, and whether Dono's application-fee reversal has been processed (proportionately on a partial refund, where the Donor was not at fault).

**Dono does not hold donation funds and cannot withhold a payout.** Where the Recipient will not act and the mandate route is unavailable or inappropriate, escalate under clause 7 of the Policy; the available levers are suspension, removal, preventing future fundraising, institutional referral and reporting suspected fraud.

## Step 9 — Post-execution

1. **Update the dispute state** for the Donation to reflect the completed refund. There is one dispute state per Donation covering refund requests, card-network disputes, chargebacks and completed refunds.
2. **Notify both parties** that the refund has been executed.
3. **Record evidence of the completed refund** against the transaction — amount, reference, date, authorising person and the determination it implements.

## Step 10 — Surplus handling

Surplus is refunded by two routes that must be reconciled through **one ledger** so that nothing is refunded twice:

- **Automatic allocation** in reverse chronological order — the most recent Donor first, up to the amount they donated, then the next, until the surplus is exhausted. Surplus is **not** distributed proportionately and no Donor is guaranteed an automatic share.
- **Individual Donor claims.**

**Before allocating or paying either, check the ledger.** A Donor who has already been refunded automatically cannot claim again for the same amount, and the total refunded can never exceed the surplus.

## Step 11 — When in doubt

If the case does not fit cleanly, is genuinely finely balanced, or raises something this checklist does not cover, **escalate to Amrit rather than deciding alone**. Do not stretch a ground to fit a sympathetic case; log it as an escalation instead.

## Appeals

An appeal is brought within **ten Working Days from the date the party is notified of the decision** — not from the date it was made — and is decided under **clause 8 of the Community Guidelines**, the single appeals framework. **Dono does not undertake to reverse a refund that has already been executed.** It is reviewed by someone not substantially involved in the original decision, ordinarily Sashank where Amrit decided and Amrit where Sashank decided. Where separation is genuinely impossible, the original decision-maker may reconsider, but only as a documented second look at the original evidence and any new information. The outcome may confirm, vary or reverse the original decision.

## 1. The eleven decisions that changed Dono's model

| # | Decision | Was (v2.2) | Is (v2.3) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fee** | Payment Provider's cost + 3.5 percentage points, with a hard-coded card-category table; unused code paths contained inconsistent fee logic | **Demo: 2% + 20p. Production: 5% + 20p.** Both are identical for every card, method and country, Campaign Owner-borne unless the Donor actively covers the applicable fee. No processing-cost table appears in a contractual document | Founder instruction; F07, F08, F09; zero-payment confirmation |
| 2 | **Refund authority** | Dono determines; only the Recipient can execute; Dono holds no power | **Dono determines and may execute**, by instructing the Payment Provider to reverse the charge under an advance, disclosed, limited mandate from the Campaign Owner and Recipient. Appeal after the event | Founder instruction; F12 |
| 3 | **Verification** | Student-card image + student number + extraction + retention, plus university email | **Student status by university email; identity by the Payment Provider's government-document and face-scan check, which Dono never receives.** Student cards and Dono's own identity-document storage are removed. *Corrected in 2.3.1 — see §8.2* | Founder instruction; F41; engineering evidence |
| 4 | **Donor age** | Anyone of any age could donate | **18+, with a mandatory legal-capacity self-certification at checkout** | Founder instruction; F15, F39 |
| 5 | **Society contracting** | Society responsible; representative automatically accepts all obligations personally | **Where the Society is unincorporated the Responsible Representative is the contracting party** (the legal position), **with express limited recourse** to the funds in the Connected Account they control — uncapped only for fraud, dishonesty, deliberate misuse, misrepresentation and lack of authority | Founder instruction + decision of 6 Aug 2026; F22, F23 |
| 6 | **Society status** | A Society is a business user | **A Society is its own category, not a business user.** Business provisions apply only to genuinely commercial or incorporated users. An individual representative is a consumer | Founder instruction; F24 |
| 7 | **Campaign scope** | Commercial and entrepreneurial campaigns permitted; recurring donations and Match Windows governed features; charities could hold Connected Accounts | **Society-only beta.** A Campaign must primarily advance the Society's activities, members or legitimate objectives. Incidental third-party benefit is permitted; primarily external benefit requires an official Society initiative directly furthering its charitable, educational, sporting, cultural or community mission. Commercial and pass-through fundraising, recurring Donations and Match Windows remain prohibited. Individual Campaigns follow later | Founder instruction; F16, F17, F28, F29 |
| 8 | **Roles** | Campaign Owner, Recipient and property owner could routinely diverge | **Alignment is the default and expected position.** Divergence is exceptional and requires an express agreement signed by every affected party before publication | Founder instruction; F26 |
| 9 | **Surplus** | Reverse chronological, with an unset de minimis threshold | **No de minimis — every penny of unused money is refundable.** Reverse-chronological automatic allocation **plus** an independent right for any donor to claim their share. No pro rata | Founder instruction + decision of 6 Aug 2026; F19, F20 |
| 10 | **Trust signals** | Plainly worded factual statuses displayed on campaign pages | **No public verification, validation, eligibility or trust indicator of any kind.** Only neutral lifecycle states | Founder instruction; F57 |
| 11 | **Campaign pages** | Publicly accessible indefinitely at their direct URL | **Archived and de-indexed 24 months after closure**, with a depublication and deletion route | Founder instruction; F45 |

## 5. Payment-services perimeter analysis of the refund mandate

**Question asked:** does the delegated-authority refund model, considered as part of the overall payment flow, have implications for UK payment-services regulation?

**Analysis.**

1. **The commercial-agent exclusion is not available and is not relied on.** FCA PERG 15.5 Q34A states expressly that an online fundraising platform accepting donations and transmitting them to the intended recipient cannot use the commercial-agent exclusion, because it is not authorised to negotiate or conclude the sale or purchase of goods or services. The v2.2 suite leaned on that exclusion; **v2.3 does not.**

2. **The better analysis is that Dono provides no payment service at all.** PERG 15.5 Q33A distinguishes a platform that takes funds into an account it controls or manages and then remits them, from one that does not. **Under Stripe Connect direct charges the charge is created on the connected account. Funds never enter an account Dono controls, and Dono holds no payment account.** Stripe is the payment service provider to the connected account. Dono supplies the interface.

3. **The technical-service-provider exclusion supports this.** PERG 15.5 Q39 and Schedule 1 Part 2 of the PSRs 2017 exclude technical service providers that support the provision of payment services **without at any time entering into possession of the funds**. That describes Dono precisely.

4. **Does the refund mandate change it?** The residual argument is that instructing a reversal is a payment initiation service. Four points weigh against it: PERG's technical-service-provider carve-out excludes PIS providers, but Dono is not offering account access to a third-party account holder as a service — it is acting under a **disclosed agency mandate from its own customer**, which is an ordinary marketplace arrangement Stripe's Connect agreement expressly contemplates; the instruction relates to reversing a charge **created through the Platform itself**, not to initiating a fresh payment order against an unrelated account; Dono never comes into possession of the funds at any point; and the money can only move **from the Campaign Owner to the Donor**, never to Dono.

5. **Conclusion: the mandate does not obviously bring Dono inside the perimeter, and it materially strengthens the consumer position.** The residual risk is a payment-initiation characterisation, which is low but not nil.

6. **Drafting adopted to keep the analysis intact.** Dono "holds no payment account" (ToS 4.2). The mandate is to "**instruct the Payment Provider to reverse a charge**", not to "execute a payment" or "move funds" (ToS 13.2). The mandate is expressly limited, cannot be used for any other purpose, gives no other account access, and cannot result in Dono receiving money (ToS 13.2(a)–(b)). Dono's inability to hold, delay or divert a payout is restated (ToS 15.7).

7. **The following would change the analysis and must not be done without advice:** holding donation funds even briefly; operating a pooled account, wallet, escrow or platform balance; delaying or holding payouts; taking a security interest or reserve over connected-account balances; or moving money between connected accounts.

**Counsel confirmation is sought** — `QUESTIONS_FOR_LAWYER.md` Q1.

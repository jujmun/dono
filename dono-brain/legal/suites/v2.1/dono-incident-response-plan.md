# Data Breach Incident Response Plan — Dono

Owner: Amrit (responsible person)
Backup: [name — to be assigned]
Last updated: 30 July 2026

## Responsible person

Amrit is the named responsible person for any data breach. In Amrit's absence, [backup] should be assigned this responsibility and briefed on this plan before launch.

## 1. Detect

Any founder or team member who becomes aware of a suspected personal data breach — unauthorised access, accidental disclosure, loss of a device holding platform data, a processor (Stripe, Convex, Vercel, Resend, PostHog) reporting an incident — must notify Amrit immediately, and in any case within 24 hours of becoming aware.

## 2. Assess

Amrit (or backup) assesses, within 24 hours of notification:
- What data is affected, and how many individuals.
- Whether the data includes special category data or identity documents.
- The likely cause (technical failure, human error, third-party breach, malicious access).
- Whether the breach is likely to result in a risk to individuals' rights and freedoms (triggers ICO notification) or a high risk (triggers individual notification).

Record this assessment in a breach log (date, description, data affected, risk assessment, decision, actions taken).

## 3. Notify the ICO (where required)

If the breach is likely to result in a risk to individuals, notify the ICO within 72 hours of Dono becoming aware, via the ICO's breach reporting service. If Dono is not yet registered with the ICO at the time of a breach, notification can still be made — registration status doesn't remove this obligation. Include: nature of the breach, categories and approximate number of individuals/records affected, likely consequences, and measures taken or proposed.

If the 72-hour deadline cannot be met, notify with the information available and provide the rest in phases, explaining the delay.

## 4. Notify affected individuals (where high risk)

If the breach is likely to result in a high risk to individuals (e.g. exposed identity documents, payment data, or data that could lead to fraud or harm), notify those individuals directly and without undue delay. Explain: what happened, what data was affected, likely consequences, and what Dono is doing and what they can do to protect themselves.

## 5. Preserve evidence

Before remediating, preserve logs, access records, and any other evidence of the breach (screenshots, processor incident reports, timestamps). This supports both the ICO notification and any later investigation.

## 6. Contain and remediate

Take immediate steps to contain the breach (revoke access, rotate credentials, patch the vulnerability) and record what was done and when.

## 7. Post-incident review

Once resolved, review: how the breach happened, whether the response met the timelines above, and what should change (technical controls, process, training) to reduce recurrence. Update this plan if gaps are found.

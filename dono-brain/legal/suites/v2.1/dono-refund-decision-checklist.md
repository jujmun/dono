# Refund Decision Checklist & Consistency Notes

**Dono** | Last updated: [date] | Supports: Refund Policy

## Purpose

Refund decisions are made by whichever staff member picks up the case, not by a panel. This checklist exists so that different reviewers reach the same decision on the same facts. Work through it in order; don't skip to a conclusion.

## 1. Conflict check — do first

Before looking at the substance of the case, check whether you should be the one deciding it. Step back and hand the case to another reviewer if any of the following apply:

- You know the campaign creator or the donor personally
- You donated to, shared, or promoted this campaign yourself
- You're a founder with a personal or financial stake in this campaign's outcome

If none apply, proceed. If any apply, note the recusal (who handed it to whom, and why) alongside the case record, and don't weigh in on the decision informally afterwards.

## 2. Ground for refund — which one applies?

Identify which single ground the request falls under. If more than one arguably applies, pick the strongest and note the others considered.

- **Misrepresentation** — the campaign's stated purpose or facts were materially untrue
- **Fraud** — the campaign or the creator's identity was fabricated
- **Technical/processing error** — wrong amount charged, duplicate charge, or similar processing fault

A donor changing their mind, without one of the above, is not a ground for refund under this checklist.

## 3. Balance of probabilities

Decide on the balance of probabilities — is it more likely than not that the ground you identified actually occurred? This is not a criminal standard; you don't need certainty, and you don't need to rule out every alternative explanation. Base the decision on the evidence actually available (campaign content, messages, Stripe records, any response from the creator), not on assumption.

## 4. Materiality

Ask whether the issue would have changed a reasonable donor's decision to give, or how much they gave. A minor inaccuracy that wouldn't have affected the donation is not material and doesn't on its own support a refund. Weigh materiality against the specific donor's stated reason for donating where one is available (e.g. a comment explaining why they gave).

## 5. Causation

Confirm the link between the issue and the donation: would this donor not have donated, or donated differently, *because of* the misrepresentation, fraud, or error? A true but unrelated problem with the campaign doesn't establish causation for this donor's specific refund request.

## 6. Recording the decision

For every decision, record:
- Ground considered and why it was (or wasn't) made out on the balance of probabilities
- Materiality assessment
- Causation assessment
- Any conflict/recusal note
- Outcome and, if refused, the reason given to the donor

## 7. When in doubt

If the case doesn't cleanly fit the above — genuinely finely balanced, or raises something this checklist doesn't cover — escalate to Amrit rather than deciding alone. Don't stretch a ground to fit a sympathetic case; log it as an escalation instead.

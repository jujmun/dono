# Dono Legal Notice-and-Action Procedure

**Version:** 3.0
**Version date:** 7 August 2026
**Effective from:** on publication approval (see approval block)
**Document type:** Internal procedure
**Owner:** Amrit Kaur Rooprai (Online Safety lead). Deputy: Sashank.
**Resolves review finding:** F61
**Referenced by:** Terms of Service clause 20; Community Guidelines clauses 7–8

> **One workflow, five complaint types.** Copyright, trade marks, impersonation, defamation and privacy complaints all enter through the same route and follow the same steps. **Dono does not determine complex legal disputes and does not guarantee any outcome.**

---

## 1. Intake

1.1 A legal notice may be submitted through the report control on the content, the public reporting route (no account needed), or **joindono.team@gmail.com**. All three create the same case record.

1.2 **Required fields.** A notice is not complete until Dono has:

| Field | Required for |
|---|---|
| Complainant name and contact email | All |
| Whether the complainant is the rights holder, the person affected, or an authorised agent | All |
| The exact URL or content identifier of each item complained of | All |
| What the complaint is (copyright / trade mark / impersonation / defamation / privacy / other) | All |
| Why the content is unlawful or infringing, in the complainant's own words | All |
| Identification of the work or right relied on, and the basis of the complainant's ownership or authority | Copyright, trade mark |
| The registration number, where a registered right is relied on | Trade mark |
| The specific words or statements said to be defamatory, and why they are false | Defamation |
| Whether the complainant can be contacted by Dono's user, and (for defamation) whether they consent to their details being passed on | Defamation |
| What personal data is at issue and why publication is unlawful | Privacy |
| A statement that the information given is accurate | All |

1.3 **Incomplete notices.** Dono asks once for what is missing, identifying everything it reasonably requires in a single request. A notice that remains incomplete is closed, with reasons, and may be resubmitted. **A closed-incomplete notice is not a finding that the content is lawful.**

## 2. Triage

2.1 Every notice is triaged within **two Working Days** of being complete.

| Priority | Trigger | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 — urgent** | Child sexual abuse material; credible threat to life; terrorism content; intimate images shared without consent | Immediate removal and the emergency workflow in the Online Safety Procedures. Not handled as an ordinary notice |
| **P2 — clear** | The unlawfulness or infringement is obvious on its face — a copied photograph, an impersonated society account, a published medical record | Remove or restrict, then notify |
| **P3 — contested** | The claim is arguable but genuinely disputed — fair dealing, criticism, a truth defence, competing accounts of ownership | Assess, and consider temporary restriction under clause 3 |
| **P4 — weak or abusive** | No arguable basis, or a pattern of notices aimed at silencing a campaign | Decline, with reasons, and apply clause 6 |

## 3. Interim measures

3.1 Where a notice is credible and the potential harm is serious, Dono **may temporarily restrict or remove** the material while it assesses. Interim restriction is not a finding.

3.2 Interim restriction is proportionate: the narrowest measure that addresses the risk. A single infringing image is removed, not the whole campaign, unless the campaign itself is the problem.

3.3 The affected user is told of the restriction, the reason, and the counter-notice route **at the time it is applied**, unless notice would be unlawful or unsafe. **Their time to respond runs from when they are notified.**

## 4. Assessment and decision

4.1 A trained reviewer assesses the notice against the Terms, the Community Guidelines and applicable law, and reaches one of: **uphold** (remove or restrict), **decline** (restore or leave in place), or **partially uphold**.

4.2 **Dono is not a court.** Where a claim turns on a genuinely disputed question of law or fact that Dono cannot resolve — the truth of a statement, the scope of a licence, competing ownership claims — Dono says so, and does one of the following: leaves the content in place and tells both parties the dispute is between them; keeps a proportionate restriction where the risk of harm is serious; or takes specialist legal advice under clause 7.

4.3 The decision, the reasoning, the policy or legal ground, the reviewer and the timestamp are recorded.

## 5. Counter-notice

5.1 Where content is removed or restricted following a notice, the affected user may submit a **counter-notice** within **ten Working Days of being notified**, stating: why the material is lawful; what right or defence they rely on; and confirming the accuracy of what they say.

5.2 On receiving a counter-notice Dono: records it; passes its substance (not necessarily the user's identity) to the complainant; and reconsiders. **The reconsideration is carried out by someone who was not substantially involved in the original decision.**

5.3 Where a counter-notice is credible and the claim is contested, Dono may **restore the material** and tell both parties that Dono will take no further action and that the dispute is between them. Dono does not restore material that it considers illegal, that breaches the Community Guidelines independently of the legal claim, or where restoration would create a safety risk.

5.4 **Defamation.** Where a complaint is that content is defamatory, Dono follows the operator's procedure under **section 5 of the Defamation Act 2013** and the Defamation (Operators of Websites) Regulations 2013: it records the date of each notice, sends the required communication to the poster within the statutory period, records the poster's response and whether they consent to their details being given to the complainant, and observes each statutory deadline. Every step and date is recorded in the case, because the defence depends on the record.

## 6. Repeat infringers and abusive notices

6.1 **Repeat infringers.** Upheld notices are recorded against the account. Three upheld notices within twelve months trigger a review of the account, which may result in restriction, suspension or a permanent ban. Deliberate or large-scale infringement may trigger immediate action.

6.2 **Abusive notices.** A notice that is not upheld is **not** abusive, and no one is penalised for a good-faith notice that turns out to be wrong. What is prohibited is a notice the complainant knows to be unfounded, a reckless notice, or a pattern of notices used to harass a user or suppress a lawful campaign. Dono may decline to process further notices from a persistent abuser, and tells them so.

## 7. Specialist escalation

7.1 The following are escalated to the Online Safety lead and, where necessary, to external legal advice before a final decision: a defamation notice that is not withdrawn after the statutory steps; a court order or an injunction; a notice from a regulator or law-enforcement agency; a claim with a real prospect of litigation against Dono; and any notice where the reviewer is unsure.

## 8. Evidence preservation

8.1 On receiving a notice, Dono preserves: the content as it appeared, in the version the complainant identified; the URL and content identifier; the account and campaign identifiers; the timestamps; the notice and any counter-notice in full; every communication with both parties; the decision and its reasoning; and any action taken.

8.2 Preserved material is retained for the period in the Privacy Notice for the relevant moderation category, and for the full period of any live dispute, claim or statutory process. Access is restricted and logged. Where the material is itself unlawful, no unnecessary copies are made.

## 9. Notification

9.1 The complainant is told: that the notice was received; the outcome; the reason; and, where declined, that they may pursue their rights elsewhere.

9.2 The affected user is told (unless it would be unlawful or unsafe): that a notice was received; the substance of the claim; what action was taken; the reason; the counter-notice route; and the deadline, running from when they are notified.

9.3 **Dono does not disclose one user's identity to another as part of this process**, except where the law requires it or the Defamation Act procedure applies and the poster consents.

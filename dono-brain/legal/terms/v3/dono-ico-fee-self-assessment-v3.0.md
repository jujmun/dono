## 1. Why this record exists

Under the Data Protection (Charges and Information) Regulations 2018, a **controller** that processes personal data must pay a data protection fee to the Information Commissioner unless an exemption applies. The obligation is **not** dependent on turnover, profit or launch — it arises **as soon as the controller starts processing personal data**.

Failure to pay is a civil matter enforceable by the ICO with a penalty of up to £4,350 (150% of the top-tier fee). It is one of the cheapest and most visible compliance failures a start-up can make, and it is checkable by anyone against the public register of fee payers.

This record was an open item in the v2.3 suite (`../../../engineering/legal-launch/README.md` external-dependency table: "ICO fee registration — complete the ICO self-assessment and record the outcome"). It is now completed.

## 2. Step 1 — Is Dono a controller?

| Question | Answer |

## 3. Step 2 — Does an exemption apply?

An exemption applies only where the controller processes personal data **exclusively** for one or more of the listed purposes. A single purpose outside the list defeats the exemption entirely.

| Exempt purpose | Does Dono process **only** for this purpose? | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Staff administration | **No** | Dono has no employees at present, and in any event processes far beyond staff administration |
| Advertising, marketing and public relations | **No** | Marketing email is a minor, consent-based activity. The core processing is a donation platform |
| Accounts and records | **No** | Transaction records are only one part. Dono also publishes user-generated content, moderates it, and processes identity outcomes, reports and safeguarding records |
| **Not-for-profit purposes** | **No** | Dono is a **sole trader operating commercially**. It charges a platform fee (5% + 20p in production, 2% + 20p in demo) and the sole trader's revenue is taxable trading income. **This is the exemption most likely to be wrongly assumed** because the platform funds student and society causes — but the exemption looks at *Dono's* purpose, not its users' causes. Dono is not a not-for-profit body |
| Personal, family or household affairs | **No** | Plainly commercial |
| Maintaining a public register | **No** | Campaign pages are not a public register in the statutory sense |
| Judicial functions | **No** | Not applicable |
| Processing without an automated system | **No** | Processing is wholly automated, hosted on Convex and Vercel |
| Members of the House of Lords, elected and prospective representatives | **No** | Not applicable |

**Result: no exemption applies. A fee is payable.**

### 3.1 Additional purposes putting the matter beyond doubt

Even if a listed exemption were arguably in play, each of the following independently defeats it:

- publishing user-generated content to the public, including to children;
- pre-publication human moderation of that content;
- processing reports, moderation decisions and enforcement records containing allegations of criminal conduct (Article 10 data);
- CSEA handling and statutory reporting to the National Crime Agency;
- processing identity-check outcomes, verified names and verified dates of birth received from the Payment Provider;
- consent-based product analytics; and
- institutional referrals.

## 4. Step 3 — Which tier?

Tier is determined by **staff numbers or turnover** (Regulations 2018, as amended). The ICO's current published fees are:

| Tier | Criteria | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — micro organisations** | Maximum turnover of **£632,000** for the financial year **or** no more than **10 members of staff** | **£52** |
| Tier 2 — small and medium | Maximum turnover of £36 million **or** no more than 250 members of staff | £78 |
| Tier 3 — large | Does not meet tier 1 or tier 2 criteria | £3,763 |

*The ICO regards all controllers as eligible for tier 3 unless and until they say otherwise. Making the self-assessment is therefore how a controller avoids the top tier.*

### 4.1 Dono's position

| Factor | Position |
|---|---|
| **Members of staff** | **Nil to low single figures.** "Members of staff" is defined broadly to include employees, workers, office holders and partners, counted as the average across the financial year, with part-time staff counted as one each, and irrespective of location. Dono is operated by the sole trader with a small number of contributors. **Well under 10** |
| **Annual turnover** | **Well under £632,000.** Dono is pre-launch with **zero previous payments processed**. Revenue is the platform fee on donations, which is nil to date. VAT-threshold monitoring is separately in hand (checklist PF-08) and Dono is not VAT-registered |
| **Public authority?** | No |
| **Charity?** | **No.** Dono is a sole trader operating commercially. It is not a registered charity and the tier 1 cap for charities is not the route relied on here |
| **Small occupational pension scheme?** | No |

**Result: Tier 1 — micro organisation. Fee: £52 per year, or £47 by direct debit (an automatic £5 discount applied at the point of payment).**

The tier 1 test is satisfied on **both** limbs independently — fewer than 10 staff **and** turnover well below £632,000 — so the determination does not depend on either figure being finely judged.

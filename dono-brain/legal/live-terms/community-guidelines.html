Dono Community Guidelines

Version 3.0 · Effective 7 August 2026 Document type: Contractual (clauses 7 and 8 are open to anyone, including non-users) Supersedes version 2.3 (6 August 2026) and all earlier versions. Precedence: These Guidelines govern content, conduct, moderation, reporting, complaints and appeals across the whole suite (Terms of Service clause 1.7). Clause 8 is the single complaints and appeals framework; every other Dono document points here.

1. Purpose and scope

1.1 These Guidelines apply contractually to account holders and to anyone who submits content or uses the community features of Dono — comments, reports, campaign pages, images, videos, documents and usernames. They form part of your agreement with Dono when you take one of those actions.

1.2 The reporting, complaints and appeals routes in clauses 7 and 8 are open to anyone, including people who do not hold a Dono account and people who have never used Dono. Those clauses describe how Dono operates; they do not require you to be a party to any contract.

1.3 Dono is a platform for genuine student fundraising within a university community. Dono's public pages can be viewed by anyone of any age, even though creating an account, commenting and donating all require you to be 18 or over. These Guidelines and the moderation process apply with that audience in mind.

2. Core principles

2.1 Be honest: campaigns and comments must not mislead. Be respectful: disagreement is fine; abuse is not. Be lawful: nothing may facilitate or promote illegal activity. Be safe: do not endanger, exploit or target others.

3. Prohibited content and conduct

3.1 The following are prohibited anywhere on the Platform. Dono removes them and may suspend or ban the account responsible.

Illegal content. Content that is, or that encourages or assists, any of the following is prohibited without exception:

  • terrorism content, and fundraising for or promotion of a proscribed organisation;
  • child sexual abuse material, and any content that sexualises, grooms or exploits a child;
  • content encouraging or assisting suicide or serious self-harm;
  • intimate images shared without consent, and cyberflashing;
  • threats to kill, harassment, stalking and controlling or coercive behaviour;
  • hate offences — content that is threatening or abusive and intended to stir up hatred on the grounds of race, religion, sexual orientation, disability or transgender identity;
  • fraud and financial-services offences, including invented beneficiaries, impersonated societies, falsified evidence and coordinated fake comments;
  • proceeds of crime, money laundering and sanctions evasion;
  • the sale or supply of drugs, psychoactive substances, firearms, knives or other unlawful goods;
  • human trafficking, sexual exploitation and facilitating unlawful immigration;
  • extreme pornography;
  • animal cruelty; and
  • foreign interference offences.

Also prohibited: misleading fundraising; fundraising whose primary purpose is to benefit an outside person or organisation unless it is an eligible official Society initiative under Terms of Service clause 8.4; pass-through fundraising; commercial or entrepreneurial fundraising; campaigns offering any reward or return; duplicate or deceptive campaigns; plagiarism; harassment and abuse falling short of an offence; discrimination; impersonation; spam; sexual or pornographic content; content that is grossly offensive; dangerous or regulated activity; copyright and trade-mark infringement; unauthorised use of institutional branding; false claims of affiliation, endorsement or verification; ban evasion; and abusive reporting under clause 7.7.

3.2 What "grossly offensive" and "dangerous" mean. These are judged by what a reasonable person would think, not by whether someone was upset. Grossly offensive means content whose main effect is to degrade, dehumanise or humiliate — for example content mocking a person's disability, or celebrating a death. Dangerous means content likely to lead a reader to physical harm — for example instructions for a hazardous stunt, or a campaign promoting an activity without the safety measures it obviously requires. Strong opinions, criticism of a campaign and blunt disagreement are not prohibited.

3.3 How we enforce. We take account of how serious the content is, whether it was deliberate, whether it caused or risked harm, whether it targeted a particular person, and whether it is part of a pattern. The proportionate outcome may be no action, a warning, removing or hiding the content, restricting a feature, restricting commenting privileges, restricting the Campaign during an investigation, suspending the account, removing the Campaign, or a permanent ban. Where necessary for safety, legal compliance or enforcement, content may be removed without prior notice.

4. Sensitive personal information

4.1 Do not publish another person's medical records, diagnosis letters, passport, driving licence, criminal allegations, disciplinary findings, religious information, political opinions or sexual orientation, unless you have the legal right to do so and it is genuinely necessary.

4.2 Do not upload unnecessary sensitive material about yourself either — for example MRI scans, diagnosis letters or identity documents — where the campaign can be made without it.

4.3 Dono may remove or redact sensitive information even where the Campaign itself remains online, and may require evidence to be submitted privately rather than published.

5. Campaign integrity

5.1 Campaigns must comply with clause 8 of the Terms of Service and the applicable Campaign Terms. Do not create duplicate or deceptive campaigns, run pass-through campaigns, use Society status as a pretext to collect for an outside person or organisation, plagiarise, or claim a check, approval, verification or endorsement you do not have. An official Society initiative may benefit third parties only within the primary-purpose and mission test in Terms of Service clause 8.4.

5.2 Dono displays no verification badges, ticks, shields or trust indicators, and no status that could be read as Dono endorsing, authenticating or guaranteeing a Campaign. The only thing shown publicly is a neutral lifecycle state. Do not describe any Dono process as a verification, validation or guarantee, in your campaign, your updates or your own promotion.

5.3 Matched funding and recurring donations are not features of the Platform. Do not advertise, imply or arrange either through a Campaign.

5.4 Everything in a Campaign is reviewed by a person before it is published — the text, the images, any uploaded document, any video and every external link — and again after any change. That review checks the Campaign against these Guidelines, the Terms and the law. It is not a verification of anything the Campaign says, and it is not shown to Donors as a status or a badge (clause 5.2).

6. Comments

6.1 There is no private messaging. All comments are public and display a name. Commenting is restricted: to post a comment you must hold an account, be 18 or over, and be an approved member of the Society that owns the Campaign. You may apply to join a Society; approval is that Society's decision, not Dono's, and a Society controls who may comment on its Campaigns. Hidden-name commenting is not available. There is no blocking feature; use reporting instead.

6.2 Comments are plain text. Links, URLs, images and attachments are not permitted, and the editor and the server reject them. This is a deliberate safety choice: links and attachments are a common route for scams, phishing and harmful material, and comments are visible to people of any age.

6.3 How comment moderation works.

(a) Automated filtering before publication. Every comment passes a keyword and pattern filter before it appears. The filter blocks defined categories — racial, religious, homophobic and sexist slurs; obvious child sexual exploitation and abuse terminology; obvious suicide-encouragement phrases; and common spam and phishing patterns. The filter is a first line of defence and catches only what it is configured to catch. It is not a review, and Dono does not claim that it catches everything. Rate limiting also applies, to reduce spam. (b) Comments that pass the filter are published immediately and are moderated afterwards. (c) Every published comment carries a visible Report control (clause 7). (d) Dono may hide or remove a comment, suspend commenting privileges, or restrict or suspend an account, immediately and without prior notice where necessary for safety, legal compliance or enforcement of the Terms, these Guidelines or the law. (e) Repeat breaches may lead to temporary or permanent loss of commenting privileges or to account suspension. Dono applies configurable thresholds for repeat-offender detection. (f) You may edit or delete your own comments; the interface shows when a comment was edited. Campaign Owners may remove a comment from their own campaign but may not edit another user's wording. Dono keeps a private moderation copy and an audit log and may restore a comment where removal is misused.

7. Reporting

7.1 A report control appears on every item of user content — campaign pages, campaign updates, campaign images and videos, uploaded documents, comments and usernames. For an update, image, video or document the control appears beside that item. The control preserves the specific content version the reporter saw.

7.2 You do not need an account to report. A publicly accessible reporting route is available without logging in and asks for: the campaign or content link; the type of content; why you are concerned; whether you are personally affected; an email address if you want updates (optional); and anything else you think relevant. You may also email joindono.team@gmail.com. Reports may be submitted without identifying the reporter, although Dono cannot send updates to an anonymous reporter.

7.3 Report reasons. You do not have to decide whether a criminal offence has occurred. Choose whichever fits best:

  • illegal content;
  • scam, fraud or misleading fundraising;
  • harassment, threats or hate;
  • terrorism or violent extremism;
  • child sexual abuse or exploitation;
  • sexual or intimate images shared without consent;
  • encouraging suicide or serious self-harm;
  • drugs, weapons or other unlawful goods;
  • spam;
  • privacy or personal-information concern;
  • content harmful to children; or
  • something else.

If you are reporting suspected child sexual abuse material, do not download, copy, screenshot or attach the material. Give Dono the link or campaign identifier; a restricted specialist workflow preserves and reviews the system-held material.

7.4 Every report creates a moderation case automatically, capturing the reported content, its version, the reporter's stated reason, the timestamps and the surrounding context.

7.5 What we do with a report. Dono acknowledges the report where contact details are given, triages it by urgency, preserves the reported content and its context, and assigns it for human review against these Guidelines, the Terms and the law. A moderator may conclude either that Dono has reasonable grounds to consider the content illegal, or that legality is uncertain but the content breaches these Guidelines or the Terms; either conclusion supports action. Dono promptly removes or restricts content it determines to be illegal, records the decision and the policy or legal ground, notifies the affected user where lawful and safe, allows a counter-notice where appropriate, applies repeat-infringer controls, and reports detected child sexual exploitation and abuse content to the National Crime Agency. Reports suggesting immediate danger bypass the ordinary queue and trigger the emergency workflow.

7.6 Response targets, not guarantees. Dono operates internal priority levels and aims to meet the targets in clause 8.4. These are service targets, not guarantees. Urgent matters — an immediate risk to a person, child safety, credible threats, or suspected illegal content — are handled straight away and outside those targets.

7.7 Abusive reporting. A report we do not uphold is not abusive, and you will never be penalised for a good-faith report that turns out to be mistaken. What is prohibited is reporting content you know to be permissible, reporting recklessly, or reporting repeatedly to harass someone or disrupt a campaign.

8. Complaints and appeals — the single framework

8.1 This clause is Dono's complaints and appeals framework for content and moderation, and for every decision listed in clause 8.2. Every other Dono document points here. Complaints about fees, technical faults, delays or how a process was handled go to the Complaints Policy. Complaints about how Dono handles personal data go to the route in the Privacy Notice. The substantive decision on whether a refund is due is made under the Refund and Dispute Policy, and appealed here.

8.3 How to submit. Use the appeal link in the decision notice, the appeal form linked to the case, or email joindono.team@gmail.com. Say which decision or report you are challenging, why, and give any new information. Dono links the submission to the original case, records its status and deadline, and sends a receipt with a tracking reference.

8.4 Timescales. We acknowledge within five Working Days. We aim to give an outcome within 30 days of receipt. If it will take longer we tell you why and when to expect it. These are targets, not guarantees. Urgent matters are handled straight away and outside these timescales.

8.5 Who decides — independence.

(a) No moderator may review an appeal against their own moderation decision. This rule has no exceptions. (b) Every appeal, and every high-impact moderation decision — account suspension, permanent ban, removal of a Campaign, or removal of significant content — is reviewed by a different trained moderator wherever reasonably practicable. (c) Where only one trained moderator is available, the matter is escalated to another authorised founder or senior reviewer, who conducts the review. Self-review is not an available outcome. (d) Amrit Kaur Rooprai is the accountable person for content and safety decisions and is the final escalation point. Where she made the original decision, another authorised person conducts the review first. (e) Conflict-of-interest and recusal rules apply to every reviewer. For particularly complex or high-risk cases Dono may take external legal or specialist advice.

8.6 Internal escalation for urgent matters. Illegal content, child-safety concerns, credible threats to a person and other urgent safeguarding incidents follow a defined internal escalation pathway to the Online Safety lead (or, in her absence, the named deputy) immediately, regardless of the ordinary queue. The escalation, the person notified and the time are logged.

8.7 Possible outcomes. The original decision may be confirmed, varied or reversed. Where it is reversed we restore the content, the campaign's functionality or account access so far as reasonably possible, and we record the reason.

8.8 Records. Every report, complaint, appeal and moderation action is logged with: the date received; the category and reason code; the acknowledgement date; the assigned reviewer and the moderator identity; the evidence considered; the original decision and its reasons; the reviewer of any appeal and their reasoning; the policy and legal grounds; notifications sent; the outcome; the timestamps of every action; and any reversal or restoration. Audit logs record every moderator action and privileged access. Dono reviews aggregated records monthly to identify repeat offenders, linked accounts, recurring campaign patterns, service risks and moderator-quality issues.

8.9 Retention. Moderation records are retained on a risk-based schedule set out in the Retention Schedule, not for a single blanket period. Only the minimum information necessary is kept for each category.

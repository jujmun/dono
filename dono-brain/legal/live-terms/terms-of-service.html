8. Campaign rules and categories

8.1 Campaigns must not involve: misleading information; fraud; plagiarism; illegal activity; copyright infringement; false claims of affiliation; duplicate or deceptive campaigns; or any prohibited category.

8.2 Permitted purposes. Campaigns are limited to personal, community, educational and student-society purposes connected with the Campaign Owner's own activity as a student or student group. These include: academic research; educational or academic projects; project-related travel; expeditions and fieldwork; society events; society equipment and activities; and the activities of religious and cultural societies.

8.3 Commercial fundraising is not permitted. Dono does not publish a Campaign operated wholly or mainly for the purposes of a trade, business, craft, profession or entrepreneurial venture. Dono may in future support commercial campaigns under a separate legal framework with its own onboarding, trader declarations, business terms, tax warnings and consumer and marketing compliance provisions; until that framework exists, commercial fundraising is not available. No Campaign may offer a reward, product, service, financial return, repayment, priority access, discount, equity, securities, tokens, revenue share or any other consideration or inducement.

8.4 Society-purpose and third-party fundraising rule. A Society Campaign is eligible only where its primary purpose advances the activities, members or legitimate objectives of that Society. The fact that third parties also benefit does not make it ineligible. A Campaign whose primary purpose is to benefit people or organisations outside the Society is prohibited during beta unless it is an official Society initiative, formally approved under the Society's rules, that directly furthers the Society's charitable, educational, sporting, cultural or community mission and is controlled and delivered by the Society rather than operating as a pass-through.

Subject to that official-initiative exception, the following are prohibited:

(a) fundraising for another individual, household, group or organisation; (b) fundraising for a registered charity, a charitable cause, or any purpose that is charitable in substance rather than funding the Campaign Owner's own activity; (c) fundraising whose primary purpose is to benefit the general public or a section of the public without directly furthering the Society's own mission through an official Society initiative; (d) pass-through campaigns, where funds or funded assets are intended to be transferred onward to a third party; and (e) campaigns where the funded property or output is intended to become the property of anyone other than the Campaign Owner or their Society.

Worked examples. Permitted: a Society buying its own stage lighting; a Society running its own cultural festival; a sporting Society delivering an approved community coaching programme as part of its mission; or a conservation Society running its own approved public conservation project. Not permitted: collecting money to pass directly to a named charity; buying equipment for an outside organisation where the Society does not itself control and deliver the initiative; raising money for a fellow student's personal costs; or acting only as a payment conduit for disaster relief.

A campaign is not prohibited merely because the Campaign Owner's own activity happens to fall within a field the law also treats as charitable — for example education, research or the environment. The test is who the money and the funded assets are for, not what subject the activity concerns.

8.5 Other prohibited categories: alcohol-related expenditure; political campaigning; investments, loans, financial products, equity or any financial return; illegal or regulated goods or conduct; tuition fees; rent; ordinary living costs; personal hardship; medical expenses; legal expenses; retrospective reimbursement; raffles, lotteries and prize draws; pooled funds, community funds, or any arrangement under which Dono would receive or hold money; and fundraising for or for the benefit of any person or organisation subject to UK financial sanctions.

8.6 Declarations and checks. At creation the Society must declare: the Society activity, member interest or legitimate objective advanced; whether any charity, community organisation or third party is involved or will benefit; who receives the funds; who controls delivery; who owns anything purchased; the Society approval relied on; and whether the Campaign is run for a trade or business. 14. Use of funds; changes; surplus

14.1 Use of funds. Campaign funds may be used only for the purposes represented to Donors, including approved line items and reasonable directly related expenditure. Funds may be moved between line items within the same stated purpose.

14.2 Partial funding. If a Campaign does not meet its target the Campaign Owner decides whether the Project can proceed with the amount raised, provided it can still be meaningfully used for the stated purpose; otherwise refunds must be arranged.

14.3 Material Change — definition. A Material Change is any change a reasonable donor would consider important when deciding whether to donate.

14.4 Material Change — categories, notice and the spending restriction.

(a) Minor changes — no donor consent required (supplier changes, a different manufacturer, colour changes, small budget reallocations, timetable changes). (b) Material but equivalent changes — the change must be submitted to Dono before it is implemented; new Donations to the Campaign are suspended; each affected Donor is notified individually and has 14 days to request a refund (different equipment, a different contractor, significant budget reallocation, a different venue).

Dono Privacy Notice

Version 3.0 · Effective 7 August 2026 Document type: Notice — information, not contract Supersedes version 2.3 (6 August 2026) and all earlier versions. Precedence: This Notice governs privacy and data-protection matters across the whole suite (Terms of Service clause 1.7).

This is a Notice, not a contract. It provides information about how Dono handles personal data and does not create contractual rights, except where the law provides otherwise. We remain responsible for our legal obligations whether or not this Notice is contractual.

The short version.

  • Dono does not collect student cards, student numbers, government identity documents or face scans. Student status is checked by control of a university email address, and that is the only check Dono performs itself.
  • The Payment Provider checks identity using a government document and a face scan. It holds those. Dono never receives or stores either — Dono is told only the outcome, the verified name and the verified date of birth.
  • Donating is for adults only.
  • Campaign pages are archived and de-indexed 24 months after closure, not kept public for ever.
  • Moderation records are kept for risk-based periods, not a flat six years.
  • Analytics runs only if you say yes, and nothing analytics-related loads before you do.
  • Dono does not share identifiable donor information with any university, college or institution.

3. Data we collect

What we do and do not hold about your identity.

We do not collect student cards. Dono does not ask for, collect, process or store a student card, an image of a student card, or a student number. That processing has been removed from the Platform entirely.

We do not hold your identity document or your face scan. To create a Campaign or a Society you complete an identity check operated by Stripe, using a government-issued identity document and a face scan. You submit those to Stripe, and Stripe holds them as an independent data controller. They never reach Dono. What Dono receives and stores is only: whether the check passed, the verified name, and the verified date of birth. Those three items are visible to Dono administrators only, and clause 7 says how long they are kept.

If you are ever asked to upload an identity document to Dono, it is not us — please report it.

3.2A Campaign updates. Where you run a Campaign you may publish updates recording what has been spent against what was raised. 3.4 Everyone who posts: comments and content (with edit history); reports you make and reports about you; moderation records.

3.5 Visitors who only browse: server log data (clause 14.4) and, only where you consent, analytics data.

3.6 People who report content without an account: the content reported, your description of the concern, whether you say you are personally affected, and your email address if you choose to give one.

3.7 We do not collect card details. Card numbers, expiry dates and security codes go directly to the Payment Provider and never reach Dono.

5. Payment data

5.1 Payments are processed by Stripe using Stripe Connect direct charges. Stripe receives and handles your full card details. Dono does not receive or store full card numbers, expiry dates or security codes, and holds no payment account.

5.2 Dono receives and stores: the amount and currency; the payment status; Stripe's transaction, charge and connected-account identifiers; whether you covered Dono's fee and the fee amounts; your email address; your display preference; and the dispute state.

5.3 Stripe is an independent controller for payment processing, Connect onboarding, know-your-customer checks, identity verification and fraud prevention, under its own privacy notice, because it determines that processing to meet its own regulatory obligations. Dono has no KYC obligation of its own.

5.3A The identity check. The government identity document and face scan you submit for the identity check go directly to Stripe and are held by Stripe. Dono never receives, sees or stores either of them. Dono receives the outcome, the verified name and the verified date of birth, and stores those against the Campaign or Society — see clause 7 for how long. Stripe's own privacy notice governs what Stripe does with the document and the scan.

5.4 Because Donations are charged directly to the Recipient's Connected Account, Stripe may show the account holder transaction details including a Donor's name, whatever their display preference on Dono — see clause 10.

6. Age

6.1 You must be 18 or over to create an account, to run a Campaign, to act for a Society, to comment and to make a Donation. Browsing is open to everyone at any age.

6.2 For Donors, age is declared and self-certified, not verified. We ask for your date of birth and, at checkout, ask you to confirm that you are 18 or over and have the legal capacity to enter into the agreement. This is not highly effective age assurance and we do not claim it is. If we reasonably believe a Donation was made by a person under 18, we may cancel or refund it.

6.2A For people who create a Campaign or Society, the verified date of birth is the final age gate. A missing, inconsistent or under-18 result blocks creation, publication and receipt of Donations. We retain it as described in clause 7 and use it only for identity, age, fraud, dispute and legal-record purposes. An apparent error may be reviewed, but we do not manually override an under-18 result without corrected information from the Payment Provider.

6.3 Because children can still reach public pages, we operate the Platform to be safe for children by default: no private messaging, no livestreaming, no disappearing content, no recommendation feed, no profiling, no behavioural advertising, no inference of characteristics, plain-text comments with no links or attachments, and high-privacy defaults applied to everyone rather than to a subset. We have completed a children's access assessment, a children's risk assessment under the Online Safety Act 2023, and an assessment against the ICO's Age Appropriate Design Code.

6.4 A parent or guardian may contact us at clause 1.2 to ask about, correct or delete a child's data, or to request the refund of a donation made by a child.

10. Hiding your name

10.1 Choosing "hide my name" hides your name from Dono's public pages only. It is not the same as being untraceable:

  • your donation amount is still shown publicly, and a unique amount or timing can sometimes identify a donor in a small community;
  • the Payment Provider has your payment data;
  • Dono always holds your name against the payment; and
  • because your payment is charged directly to the Recipient's own Stripe account, the person or Society operating that account may be able to see the transaction details Stripe shows them, which can include your name. Dono cannot prevent that and does not claim to.

We explain this when you choose the option. Changing the setting affects future display only. In a dispute, Dono withholds your identity from the Campaign Owner and discloses it only where required or permitted by law, and the Campaign Terms prohibit a Campaign Owner from using payment information to identify, contact or pressure you.

11. Receipts and other people's data in what you upload

11.1 Campaign Owners submit receipts and evidence privately to Dono; these are never published or shown to Donors. Receipts often contain personal data about people other than the uploader — a supplier's staff, a delivery recipient, a co-purchaser.

11.2 We require receipts to be redacted before upload. Guidance is shown before every upload. Users must remove anything not needed to verify the purchase: names, addresses, email addresses and telephone numbers of individuals; delivery addresses; payment-card details, security codes and full bank or sort-code details; unrelated purchases on the same document; and special category information unless inherent in the campaign purpose. Users must keep supplier names, item descriptions, prices, dates, order references and VAT details, which we need.

11.3 Non-compliant receipts. A receipt containing unnecessary personal data is rejected and moved to a short-retention quarantine, and automatically deleted after 30 days. The uploader is asked to submit a properly redacted version. Deletion is logged and auditable.

11.4 What we keep from an accepted receipt is the minimum needed for verification: supplier, item, amount, date and reference. We may ourselves redact or remove unnecessary personal data from an upload.

11.5 Third parties named on a receipt — our Article 14 position. Where we hold information about you only because it appeared on a receipt someone else uploaded, we process it solely to check that campaign funds were spent as described. We do not use it for anything else, do not share it, and do not use it to contact you.

We do not normally give each such person individual notice. We have completed and documented a UK GDPR Article 14 assessment concluding that individual notice would involve disproportionate effort, because: Dono usually holds no reliable contact details for the person; the data is incidental, minimal and not used to make any decision about them; contacting them would require processing more of their data than we already hold, and would itself create a privacy intrusion; and the safeguards we apply substantially reduce any impact. Those safeguards are: mandatory pre-upload redaction guidance; rejection and 30-day quarantine of non-compliant uploads; minimisation on acceptance; restricted, logged access; defined retention; and making this Notice publicly available, which is the appropriate measure we take in place of individual notice. Where we do hold contact details for a person named on a receipt and the risk to them warrants it, we give direct notice.

If you believe your data appears on a receipt we hold, contact us at clause 1.2 and we will deal with it.

12. Special category and criminal-offence data

12.1 Some campaigns, comments or reports may contain special category data (for example health, disability, religion or sexual orientation) or criminal-offence data (an allegation, suspicion or record of unlawful conduct). Dono does not ask for this and designs no feature to collect it. Where it arises, we do not rely on one blanket justification — the basis depends on whether the data is something you chose to disclose about yourself or something alleged about you by someone else, and on the specific purpose (safeguarding, fraud, moderation, a legal claim, or a statutory report).

12.2 What you disclose about yourself. Where a campaign, campaign update or comment you publish reveals something about your own health, disability, religion, sexual orientation or similar characteristic, we process it because you made it public yourself, on the basis that it is data manifestly made public by you. We never use this basis for anything you have not made public — not a private draft, not a receipt, and not anything someone else says about you.

12.3 What is alleged about you by someone else. A safeguarding concern, a fraud report, a moderation decision, or a report to the National Crime Agency or another authority is treated differently from your own disclosures, because it is not information you chose to share. Each is processed only where a specific, matched condition applies — safeguarding concerns under the condition built for protecting children and individuals at risk; fraud and dishonesty concerns under the condition built for protecting the public against dishonesty; Dono's own moderation decisions and statutory reports under the condition for preventing or detecting unlawful acts; and anything relevant to a legal claim under the condition for legal claims. Where none applies, see clause 12.5.

12.4 We do not infer characteristics from your behaviour. We do not derive, tag or record any inference about your health, religion, political views, sexual orientation or other protected characteristic from your donation history or browsing, and we build no profiles of that kind.

12.5 We do not act on unsupported allegations. Where a report about you has no connection to the Platform, is speculative, has no stated basis, or is being used to harass or pressure you, we do not open a case, do not investigate it, and do not retain its substance — we keep only a minimal log that a report was declined, for 12 months. Where a report does meet the bar for a case to be opened, it is recorded and described as an allegation, never as an established fact, unless and until it is upheld.

12.6 Correcting the record. If you are the subject of an allegation Dono has acted on, you may ask us to correct a factual inaccuracy in how it is recorded, and — where telling you would not itself undermine a safeguarding matter, an investigation or a statutory report — you can find out that a decision was made about you and challenge it through the appeals process in clause 8 of the Community Guidelines. Access to allegation and safeguarding records is restricted and logged, and each is kept only for the period in clause 7.1, with reports to the National Crime Agency held on two separate, shorter clocks.

12.7 The full condition-by-condition analysis, retention periods and safeguards are documented in Dono's internal Appropriate Policy Document.
